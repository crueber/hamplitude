## What it is

Running a station away from the mains means running it from batteries, charged from a solar panel, a vehicle or a generator. The questions to answer are: how much energy does the station use, how much can the battery supply, and how will it be recharged?

## How long will it last?

A battery's size is its capacity in **amp-hours** (Ah). Divide the usable amp-hours by the average current to get hours:

<Formula label="Runtime estimate">usable Ah ÷ average current (A) = hours</Formula>

<PortablePower_Runtime />

Average current mixes the small receive current with the large transmit current by how often you transmit. A QRP radio at 5 W on a 20 Ah lead-acid battery can run for many hours; a 100 W radio at 25% transmit time will drain the same battery quickly. These are **estimates**: SSB speech draws less than the peak figure, while digital modes and FM draw near peak all the time.

## Choosing a battery

| | Sealed lead-acid | Lithium iron phosphate (LiFePO4) |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | heavy | much lighter for the same usable energy |
| Usable capacity | about half, for long life | most of it, with a built-in protection board |
| Voltage as it discharges | sags steadily | stays nearly flat, then drops quickly |
| Charging | simple charger | needs a charger or controller made for it |

Both are nominally 12 V and fall within what most radios accept (check the manual). A lead-acid battery that is nearly flat may sag too low under a transmit peak, so the radio's output drops, which is the voltage-drop problem again; see <Ref to="shack-power-supplies" />. See <Ref to="batteries" /> for the chemistry.

## Solar charging

A silicon cell makes about 0.5 V, so a "12 V" panel is many cells in series, with an open-circuit voltage well above 12 V. Connected directly to a battery it could overcharge it, so a **charge controller** sits between them. A series diode stops the battery discharging through the panel at night.

<SolarWiring />

A panel's rated watts are for full sun at the best angle; real output is usually lower. A rough sizing check is panel watts ÷ about 14 V for the maximum charging current. Partial shade can cut a panel's output sharply.

## Safety

<BatteryHazards />

- **Fuse the positive lead within inches of the battery**, sized to the wire. A battery can deliver hundreds of amps into a short, enough to melt wire and start a fire.
- Remove rings and metal watches around terminals, and cover exposed terminals when moving a battery.
- Charge a battery only with a charger made for its chemistry, at a sensible rate. Charging or discharging too fast overheats it, and lithium cells can catch fire.
- A lead-acid battery can release hydrogen when charging, so charge it somewhere ventilated and away from sparks.
- Run a generator outdoors, well away from tents, windows and doors: its exhaust contains deadly carbon monoxide.
- Check airline rules before travelling with lithium batteries.
