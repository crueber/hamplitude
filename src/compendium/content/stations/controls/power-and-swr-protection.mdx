## What it is

The power control sets how much RF the radio's final amplifier delivers. **SWR protection** is the circuit that turns that power down when the antenna system is a poor match, so the transistors in the final stage are not overstressed.

## Setting your power

Power is a control, not a goal. Turn it down for a local contact; turn it up only when the path needs it. Doubling power is only 3 dB, about half an S unit at the far end (see <Ref to="decibels" />), so extra watts help far less than a better antenna.

- Speech peaks are brief, so SSB heats the final amplifier less than **continuous-carrier modes** such as FM, RTTY or digital. Lower the power for those, as the manual advises.
- Use a low-power **tune** mode when adjusting an <Ref to="antenna-tuners">antenna tuner</Ref> or an amplifier, so the radio is not sending full power into a bad match.
- A low supply voltage lowers the radio's output: see <Ref to="shack-power-supplies" />.

## Why SWR matters to the radio

A final amplifier is designed to see about 50 Ω. A mismatched antenna reflects part of the wave back, and the amplifier then sees a different load. The extra current or voltage can overheat or destroy its transistors. The fraction reflected depends only on SWR:

| SWR | Power reflected | Mismatch loss |
|---|---|---|
| 1:1 | 0 % | 0 dB |
| 1.5:1 | 4 % | 0.18 dB |
| 2:1 | 11 % | 0.51 dB |
| 3:1 | 25 % | 1.25 dB |
| 5:1 | 44 % | 2.55 dB |

Moderate SWR is not a catastrophe: at 2:1 the mismatch itself costs only about half a decibel. The danger is mainly to the radio. See <Ref to="swr-and-reflections" /> for the mechanism.

## How the protection works

A **directional coupler** inside the radio samples the forward and reflected waves and calculates SWR. A control circuit then lowers the drive to the final amplifier, a response called **foldback**, or shuts the transmitter down altogether. Radios also protect against heat and overcurrent.

## Pitfalls

- **Never transmit with no antenna or load attached.** An open connector is the worst mismatch there is. Use a <Ref to="dummy-loads">dummy load</Ref> for testing.
- A <Ref to="antenna-tuners">tuner</Ref> at the radio makes the radio see 1:1, but any mismatch beyond the tuner is still there on the feed line.
- Protection is a backstop, not a tuning method. A radio that keeps folding back is telling you the antenna, a connector or the feed line needs attention.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Check before you key up">Wrong band, a loose PL-259 or a switch between positions are the usual causes of a sudden high SWR. Lower the power while you find out.</Callout>
