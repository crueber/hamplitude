## What it is

A transceiver is a transmitter and receiver sharing one box, one frequency control and one antenna. An HF transceiver covers the shortwave bands, usually 160 m through 10 m, often 6 m too, and is the usual heart of a home station. It is the radio you use to work other continents.

## What to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Receiver performance | Crowded bands put many strong signals near the one you want. Dynamic range and selectivity decide whether you hear it cleanly; see <Ref to="receiver-performance" /> |
| Filters and DSP | Narrow CW and SSB filters, notch and noise reduction make weak signals readable; see <Ref to="radio-filters" /> |
| Built-in antenna tuner | Matches a modest mismatch so the rig sees a load it can drive. It cannot fix a poor antenna |
| Spectrum display | Shows activity around you at a glance, a feature of <Ref to="sdr-transceivers" /> and many hybrids |
| Computer interface | USB for <Ref to="cat-control" /> and digital-mode audio |
| Amplifier and key jacks | Keying line and ALC input for a <Ref to="linear-amplifiers">linear amplifier</Ref>, plus key or paddle jack |
| Split, RIT, dual receive | Working DX and contests; see <Ref to="rit-xit-and-split" /> |
| Cooling and duty cycle | Digital modes and RTTY transmit continuously, which heats the final amplifier. Run well below full power for those |

Among full-size rigs, output power varies little. The differences are mostly receiver quality, filtering, display and convenience.

## In practice

- Use the lowest power that makes the contact. Most contacts do not need 100 W, and a clean signal at lower power beats a distorted one at full power.
- Adjust mic gain and processing so the ALC stays in its range; see <Ref to="speech-processing-and-mic-gain" />.
- Some compact HF rigs are built for vehicles, with a detachable front panel; see <Ref to="mobile-operation" />.
- Know your license privileges before transmitting: bands and modes differ by class. Output power also matters for <Ref to="rf-exposure" />.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Antenna first">A better antenna improves both transmit and receive. A modest rig on a good antenna usually outperforms an expensive rig on a poor one.</Callout>
