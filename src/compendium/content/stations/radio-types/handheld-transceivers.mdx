## What it is

A handheld transceiver, or HT, is a battery-powered, pocket-sized radio with its antenna attached. Most are FM transceivers for the 2 m (144 MHz) and 70 cm (430 MHz) bands. They are a common first radio because they are portable and, once programmed, reach local repeaters.

## Where it fits

<HandheldTransceivers_Chooser />

An HT is the right tool for local, pocket-sized communication. It is the wrong tool for anything that needs range, because of what you give up to make it small.

## Why range is limited

<Loading />

- **Antenna.** A flexible antenna is much shorter than the quarter-wavelength it replaces, so it is a compromise (see <Ref to="handheld-antennas" /> and <Ref to="loaded-and-short-antennas" />). It is also held low, near your body, which absorbs and detunes it.
- **Power.** A few watts, set by what a small pack can deliver. Low power also extends battery life.
- **Height.** At VHF and UHF, signals travel roughly line of sight, so a radio at head height reaches little farther than the nearest hill or building.

The simplest big improvement is an outside antenna: an external connector lets you use a vehicle or home antenna with the same radio.

## What to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Programming | Entering repeaters by keypad is slow; computer programming and memory channels save time |
| Tones | CTCSS and DCS are needed to open many repeaters; see <Ref to="squelch-and-tones" /> |
| Battery | Capacity sets runtime. A spare pack or battery case helps in the field |
| Receiver quality | Cheap receivers can overload near strong transmitters, causing noise or silence |
| Digital voice | Some HTs add DMR, D-STAR or Fusion; these families do not talk to each other directly |
| Ruggedness | Water and dust resistance, a screen you can read outdoors |
| Extras | GPS and APRS, broadcast and airband receive, speaker-mic connector |

## In practice

- Keep the antenna away from your face while transmitting, use low power when it is enough, and use a speaker-mic so the radio is held away from your head on long transmissions. Higher power near the body means higher exposure; see <Ref to="rf-exposure" />.
- Use the charger made for the pack. Lithium batteries can be a fire hazard if they are shorted, damaged or charged with the wrong equipment; see <Ref to="battery-and-fire-safety" />.
- Programming a radio for frequencies you are not licensed for, or leaving it set to a repeater's wrong offset, can cause interference. See <Ref to="memories-and-scanning" /> and <Ref to="repeaters" />.
