## What it is

QRP means low power: by long-standing convention 5 W or less on CW, and 10 W PEP or less on SSB. A QRP radio is a small transceiver built around that power level, with a low current drain, so it runs from a small battery and fits in a pack. The same Q signal, "QRP?", asks "shall I reduce power?".

## What you give up

<QrpRadios_Decibels />

Signal strength depends on the ratio of powers, in decibels. Going from 100 W to 5 W is a factor of 20, which is 10 × log₁₀(20) ≈ 13 dB, about two S-units on a calibrated meter. The other station hears you that much weaker, and noise and fading hurt more.

You can win much of it back:

- **Narrow modes.** A CW receive filter is a few hundred hertz wide, an SSB filter about 2.4 kHz. Less bandwidth means less noise, and a CW signal often readable when SSB is not. Digital modes such as FT8 work well at low power for the same reason.
- **A good antenna.** An efficient, well-placed antenna adds more than extra watts would; see <Ref to="portable-field-antennas" />.
- **Timing.** Choose bands and times when the path is open; see <Ref to="muf-and-luf" />.

## What to look for

| Feature | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Size and weight | Every ounce is carried up a hill |
| Receive current | Sets battery life, since most of the time you are listening |
| Bands and modes | A single-band CW rig is small and simple; a multimode multiband rig is more flexible |
| Receiver quality | Small rigs vary a lot; a quiet, selective receiver matters on a crowded band |
| Display and controls | Readable in sunlight, usable with gloves |
| Built-in keyer or tuner | Saves carrying extra parts; check what is included |
| Voltage range | Accepts a battery's range from fresh to nearly flat |

## In practice

QRP rigs are the standard companions of summit and park activations (<Ref to="summits-on-the-air" />, <Ref to="parks-on-the-air" />), backpacking and emergency kits. Some operators build them from kits (<Ref to="kits-and-homebrew" />), and there is a whole contest class for low-power stations. At home, QRP is a way to learn how much a good antenna and good operating can do.

Lower power does not mean no safety: batteries can start fires if shorted, and an antenna close to people still deserves respect; see <Ref to="battery-and-fire-safety" /> and <Ref to="portable-power" />.
