## What it is

PSK31 is a keyboard-to-keyboard chat mode: you type, the other operator sees it appear almost as you type it. It sends text on a single, very narrow signal, so it works well with low power and simple antennas, and many conversations fit in a tiny slice of a band. The name means **P**hase **S**hift **K**eying at about **31** baud.

## How it works

<T8D_Keying />

CW switches a carrier on and off. PSK31 leaves the tone on and flips its **phase**: a reversal of phase is one value of a bit, no reversal is the other. A sharp reversal would splatter energy across the band, so PSK31 fades the amplitude smoothly down to zero at each reversal and back up.

<E8C_PskShaping />

That smooth shaping is why the signal stays about 31 Hz wide. It also means a clean transmitter matters: an overdriven or distorted PSK31 signal grows extra traces on the waterfall and wastes its narrow-band advantage.

## Varicode

<E2E_Varicode />

Instead of giving every character the same number of bits, Varicode gives **common letters short codes** and rare ones long codes. Lower-case text therefore goes faster than text full of capitals, digits and symbols, because those need longer bit sequences. Capitals are not sent with more power, only with more bits.

## In practice

- The signal is a thin line on the <Ref to="digital-mode-software">waterfall</Ref>: click on it to tune. The software follows small drifts, but the narrow signal needs a stable radio.
- It uses an SSB radio with computer audio, normally upper sideband. Run modest power and keep audio levels low enough that the radio's ALC is not working.
- With no error correction, a weak or fading signal turns into gibberish. The basic mode is best at moderate strength with a clean path; modes built for the weakest signals appear in <Ref to="ft8-and-ft4" /> and <Ref to="mfsk-and-olivia" />.
- Contacts are conversational: call CQ, then trade names, locations and signal reports as in <Ref to="ragchewing" />.
