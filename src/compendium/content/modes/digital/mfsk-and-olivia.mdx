## What it is

A group of keyboard modes that send text on **many tones** with built-in error correction. They are built to survive the conditions that wreck RTTY and PSK31: fading, interference, static crashes and signals below the noise. They are slower and typically wider, but they keep a conversation going when the simple modes cannot.

## How they work

**MFSK** is multiple frequency-shift keying: instead of two tones (mark and space), the signal picks one tone out of many each symbol, so each symbol carries several bits. **Olivia** and **MT63** go further and send each character spread across many tones and over time, with error-correcting code across the whole group.

If a burst of static or a fade wipes out some symbols, there is still enough left to reconstruct the character.

This is **forward error correction (FEC)**, the second approach in the diagram above: the receiver fixes errors using the redundancy it was sent. There is no return channel, so it works for one-way broadcast and group chat as well as two-way conversation.

## What it costs

- **Speed.** Text is slow. Olivia in particular is far slower than PSK31.
- **Delay.** Characters arrive in blocks, only after the whole block has been received. A reply feels like a short wait.
- **Bandwidth.** Robust configurations take wider slices of the band than PSK31. MT63 is wide; Olivia lets you pick between narrower and wider settings, and the narrower ones are slower.

## Choosing one

| Situation | Leans toward |
|---|---|
| Fading, flutter, noisy HF paths | Olivia or MFSK16 |
| Lots of static crashes or impulse noise | MT63 |
| Clean, strong, narrow-band signals | PSK31 or RTTY |

Both stations must agree on the mode and its settings, so operators arrange them in advance or announce them on the air.
