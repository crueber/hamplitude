## What it is

Simplex means both stations transmit and receive on **the same frequency**, taking turns, with no repeater in between. It is the simplest way to use a radio: no infrastructure, no tones, no offsets. It is also how radios keep working when a repeater, or the power to it, fails.

## How far will it reach?

VHF and UHF travel in straight lines, so range is set mainly by how high each antenna is. Each antenna can "see" about 1.41 × √height (in feet) miles to its radio horizon, and the two add. A handheld at head height sees about 3 miles; an antenna 50 feet up sees about 10.

<Simplex_Range />

That is a best case: hills, buildings and dense trees shorten it, and a good antenna on a high point often beats a high-power radio at street level. Moving a few feet, or standing at a window, can make the difference.

## Using it

- **Calling frequencies are for calling.** Make contact on 146.520 MHz, then move to a clear simplex channel (the <Ref to="band-plans" /> lists them) so the calling frequency stays free.
- **Listen first**, then ask if the frequency is in use. Nobody owns a frequency.
- **To call:** "CQ, this is W1ABC, listening on 146.52." Use phonetics for your call sign if conditions are poor.
- **Identify** at least every 10 minutes and at the end of a contact.
- Use a **vertical antenna**, matching the near-universal vertical polarization on FM.

## Why bother?

<Linked />

- **It keeps repeaters free.** If you can talk direct, don't tie up a machine that others in a wider area need.
- **It still works when infrastructure doesn't.** Emergency groups rely on simplex for that reason, and often assign simplex channels in advance.
- **It teaches you your radio's real range**, which is worth knowing before you need it.
