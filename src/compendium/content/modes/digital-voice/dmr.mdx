## What it is

DMR (Digital Mobile Radio) is a digital voice standard created for business and industry and widely adopted by amateurs. It is known for two features: **two conversations time-share one channel**, and **talkgroups** that let many repeaters carry the same conversation across a region or the world.

## Two slots on one channel

A DMR repeater sends and receives in short bursts. Bursts alternate between **slot 1** and **slot 2**, so two independent calls use one 12.5 kHz channel, each at half the airtime. Handheld radios transmit only during their slot, which also eases the load on the battery.

## Getting in, and choosing who you talk to

Think of a DMR repeater as a building with a door and many rooms.

<Dmr />

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Color code** | Access code: your radio must match the repeater's. It stops a repeater answering signals meant for another on the same frequency. |
| **Time slot** | Which of the two bursts you use; the repeater's pages say which talkgroups are on which slot. |
| **Talkgroup** | A numbered conversation. Your radio only plays what it is set to hear. |
| **Radio ID** | A number identifying your radio on the network, registered to your call sign. |
| **Code plug** | The file loaded into the radio holding its channels, contacts and talkgroups. |

Because everything must match, a DMR radio is **programmed with a computer**, not just a keypad. Mistakes are silent: the wrong color code or slot means the repeater simply ignores you.

## In practice

- Listen before you key up: a talkgroup may be in use even if your radio is not set to it.
- Wide-area talkgroups carry traffic from many repeaters at once. Keep calls short and leave room for others.
- A **private call** goes to one radio ID rather than a talkgroup.
- Amateur DMR networks link repeaters over the internet. A **hotspot** gives you a low-power connection at home; see <Ref to="hotspots-and-gateways" />.
- DMR radios from different makers generally interoperate on the air, though their programming software differs.
