## What they are

P25 and NXDN are digital voice standards created for **professional land mobile radio**: public safety, utilities, transport. A few amateurs use them because commercial radios are widely available second-hand, and because many multi-mode hotspots can carry them. Neither was designed for amateur radio, so some features do not fit it.

## Sharing the spectrum

Digital voice squeezes more calls into the same spectrum in different ways. The two standards take different routes.

<P25AndNxdn_Access />

- **P25 Phase 1** sends one call per 12.5 kHz channel. **Phase 2** adds two time slots in the same 12.5 kHz, as <Ref to="dmr" /> does.
- **NXDN** can use channels of 6.25 kHz, so two calls fit in the space of one 12.5 kHz channel, or 12.5 kHz where wider is wanted.

P25 Phase 1 and NXDN use four-level frequency shifts (see <Ref to="system-fusion" />). All of them rely on a vocoder, as in <Ref to="d-star" />.

## Getting in

| | P25 | NXDN |
|---|---|---|
| Access code | NAC, a code in the signal; a receiver set to one NAC ignores others | RAN, similar idea |
| Who to hear | a **talkgroup** ID | a **talkgroup** or unit ID |
| Origin | Public-safety standard | Commercial two-way radio |

Amateur networks that carry these modes work much like the DMR ones: repeaters or hotspots link over the internet and talkgroups pick the conversation. See <Ref to="internet-linking" />.

## Amateur-radio cautions

- **No encryption.** Commercial P25 and NXDN radios can scramble audio. Part 97 forbids encoding messages to obscure their meaning, so any such feature must be off.
- **Check the band.** Professional radios are made for other services and may not cover or be programmable on amateur channels.
- **Other digital modes cannot decode them.** A receiver must understand the same standard and vocoder.
