## What it is

A **long wire** is a wire at least about a wavelength long, usually fed at one end. A **random wire** is a wire of any convenient length, with a tuner or matching box making up the difference. Both are the cheapest antennas to build, and both ask more of the station than they seem to.

## Long wires: lobes, not a beam

<E9C_LongWire />

As an unterminated wire grows longer in wavelengths, extra lobes appear and the main lobes swing toward the wire's own axis. Gain over a dipole grows slowly, and the pattern has many lobes and nulls, so a long wire is not a precise beam. Adding a terminating resistor at the far end turns a bidirectional pattern into a unidirectional one; this is how a rhombic or <Ref to="beverage-antennas">Beverage</Ref> works.

## Random wires: any length, but not any impedance

<LongAndRandomWires_FeedZ />

Any wire can be tuned, but the feed-point impedance depends on its length in wavelengths. The curve is a schematic from an ideal wire: it repeats every half wavelength and is very high near each multiple of a half wave. A tuner can match it, but high impedance means high voltage, and loss in the tuner and any feed line rises. Pick a length that avoids half-wave multiples on the bands you use; no one length is good on every band.

## The station is part of it

<G9B_RandomWire />

With the wire connected straight to the radio there is no coax shield to keep RF contained, so station equipment may carry significant RF current. You see it as a tingling mic, a hot case or a computer that glitches when you transmit. Keep this in check by:

- Supplying a **counterpoise**: one or more wires, or a ground connection, at the feed point, rather than letting your cables act as one.
- Putting the tuner at the wire's feed point, or using a transformer there (often 9:1) and running coax from it, with a <Ref to="common-mode-chokes">common-mode choke</Ref>.
- Remembering that a tuner makes the radio happy but does not reduce losses in the wire's system; see <Ref to="antenna-tuners" />.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Wire and power lines">Never string or throw a line anywhere near power lines. Keep the feed end of a high-power wire out of reach, as it can burn.</Callout>
