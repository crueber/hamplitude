## What it is

Two equal lengths of wire, a quarter-wavelength each, fed at the centre. Together they span half a wavelength, hence the name. It is the simplest efficient antenna, and the yardstick other antennas are measured against.

## How it works

<HalfWaveDipole_Standing />

A wave travels to the end of the wire and reflects, setting up a standing wave. At the ends the current has nowhere to go, so it is zero and the voltage is highest. In the middle it is the other way round: high current, low voltage.

That is why the centre is the natural place to feed it: it presents a low impedance, close enough to 50 Ω coax. Feeding at an end instead means high voltage and low current, a very high impedance; see <Ref to="end-fed-half-wave" />.

## Length

<G9B_DipoleLength />

Radio waves travel slightly slower along a wire than through free space, and the wire's ends add a little capacitance. Both effects make the antenna electrically longer than it looks, so the wire is cut about 5% short of a true half wavelength. That is the 468 in the formula, rather than 492.

| Band | Frequency | Total length |
|---|---|---|
| 40 m | 7.15 MHz | 65.5 ft (about 20 m) |
| 20 m | 14.2 MHz | 33.0 ft (about 10 m) |
| 2 m | 146 MHz | 38.5 in |

<Callout kind="tip" title="Trim in steps">Cut a little long, then shorten both legs equally while watching the SWR dip. Cutting makes the resonant frequency go up; you can't put wire back.</Callout>

## Pattern

<DipoleDonutPattern />

The antenna radiates best at right angles to the wire and not at all along it, like a donut with the wire through the hole. Seen from above, that is a figure-8.

Height above ground matters as much as the antenna itself:

<G9B_DipoleHeight />

Low (well under a quarter wavelength) it sends most energy upwards, good for contacts within a few hundred miles. Raised toward a half wavelength or more, more energy goes out at low angles, better for distant stations.

## In practice

- Feed with coax through a 1:1 balun or <Ref to="common-mode-chokes">common-mode choke</Ref>, so RF stays off the outside of the cable.
- It works as a flat line, or drooped into an <Ref to="inverted-v" /> if you have one tall support.
- It is naturally narrow-band: a few percent of the frequency. Multiband versions use <Ref to="multiband-dipoles">fan or trap designs</Ref>.
- Nearby trees, metal and buildings pull the resonant frequency down, so a real antenna is often a bit shorter than the formula says.
