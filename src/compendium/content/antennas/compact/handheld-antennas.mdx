## What it is

The antenna on a handheld transceiver (HT) is a compromise: it has to be short enough to carry, flexible enough to survive a pocket and still radiate. The standard "rubber duck" is a coil of wire inside a flexible sleeve. It works, but a better antenna is often the easiest way to improve an HT.

## Choices drawn to scale

<HandheldAntennas_Lengths />

- **Rubber duck.** A helix (a coil) wound along a short flexible core, loaded to look like a quarter-wave. It is compact and rugged and its efficiency is low, as with any <Ref to="loaded-and-short-antennas">short antenna</Ref>.
- **Quarter-wave whip.** A straight piece of wire or telescoping rod. Efficient, but the radio's case and your hand act as the other half of the antenna.
- **Half-wave telescopic.** A longer antenna with a small matching network at its base. It relies less on the radio body and often works better.
- **5/8-wave whip.** More gain on paper in the open; see <Ref to="five-eighths-and-colinears" />.

On the signal side, longer is generally better until size becomes a nuisance. The difference between a duck and a good full-size antenna can be several dB.

## The radio is part of the antenna

<T9A_Loading />

A handheld on its own has no ground plane. Your hand, the case and the battery pack form a rough counterpoise, so how you hold the radio changes the signal. A hand wrapped around the case or the antenna and a body close to the radio absorb RF. Hold the radio upright and, if you can, away from your body. A metal vehicle body screens a handheld inside it, which is why people switch to an outside antenna in a car.

## Getting more from an HT

- **Get higher.** Height beats antenna length. A few feet up a hill or a building matters more than any whip.
- **Use an external antenna.** A magnetic-mount whip on a car roof, a roll-up J-pole from a window, or a small Yagi, all through a short coax. See <Ref to="j-pole-and-slim-jim" />.
- **Keep the coax short.** Thin coax wastes more of the signal at VHF and UHF, so use the shortest, best-quality run you can.
- **Check connectors.** Match male and female and standard or reverse-polarity SMA before buying.
- **Mind exposure.** Keep the antenna away from your face and eyes and don't hold or touch it while transmitting; see <Ref to="rf-exposure" />.
