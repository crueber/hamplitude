## What it is

Radials are wires laid out from the base of a vertical. They act as the antenna's other half, carrying the return current back to the feed point so it does not have to travel through the soil. Without them a <Ref to="quarter-wave-vertical">quarter-wave vertical</Ref> cannot work properly.

## Why a vertical needs them

<RadialsAndGroundPlanes_Return />

Current that flows up the element has to return to the transmitter. Ordinary soil is a poor conductor, so on its own it behaves like a resistor in series with the antenna. Power that goes into that resistance is turned into heat instead of radio waves.

<RadialsAndGroundPlanes_Loss />

The antenna's radiation resistance is only about 36 Ω, so even 10 Ω of ground loss wastes more than a fifth of your power (36 ÷ 46 = 78% efficient). The goal of a ground system is to make the loss resistance a small fraction of 36 Ω.

## Two kinds of ground system

| | Ground-mounted | Elevated |
|---|---|---|
| Layout | Many radials on or just under the soil | A few radials, a quarter-wave long, some height above ground |
| How many | Dozens are common; each extra one helps less | Typically 2 to 4 per band |
| Why | Spreads the return current over a large area to cut soil loss | The radials are the entire counterpoise, so soil matters much less |
| Trade-off | Lots of wire; tuned length matters little | One set of radials per band; supports needed |

Gains from extra ground-mounted radials flatten out: going from a handful to a few dozen helps a lot, going from dozens to hundreds helps little. Soil quality matters too. Seawater is an excellent ground and poor dry soil is a bad one.

<G9B_GroundPlane />

Elevated radials also set the feed impedance. Flat they leave it near 36 Ω, and sloping them downward raises it toward 50 Ω, a better match for coax. Neither the exact number of radials nor the exact angle is critical; plan on adding wire until the ground loss stops mattering.

## In practice

- On VHF and UHF a handful of short rods is enough: they are only inches long, and they form a good ground plane.
- On a car, the metal body is the ground plane; see <Ref to="mobile-whips-and-loading" />.
- Radials lying on the grass are a trip and mower hazard: bury them, pin them down or mark them.
- Radials are different from a safety ground. A radial system does not protect against lightning; see <Ref to="lightning-and-grounding" />.
