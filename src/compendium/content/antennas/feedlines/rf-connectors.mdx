## What it is

An RF connector joins a cable to a radio, antenna or another cable. A good one is part of the transmission line: it keeps the impedance steady, holds the shield continuous and survives the weather. A bad choice or a bad installation is a common cause of loss, odd SWR and intermittent faults.

## The common families

<RfConnectors_Gallery />

| Connector | Coupling | Typical use | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **UHF** (PL-259 plug, SO-239 socket) | threaded | HF and VHF radios, amplifiers, mobile | cheap and robust, but the impedance is not held constant, so it is a poor choice at UHF and up; not weatherproof on its own |
| **N** | threaded | outdoor runs, repeater sites, UHF and microwave | holds a constant impedance; sealed versions resist the weather; larger and heavier |
| **BNC** | bayonet, quarter turn | test gear, handhelds, short jumpers | quick to connect; light duty |
| **TNC** | threaded | mobile and vibration-prone installs, low microwave | like a BNC but locked by threads |
| **SMA** | small threaded hex nut | handhelds, modules, microwave | small and light; delicate and low power; wears with repeated use |

Other types you will meet: F connectors on 75 Ω TV cable, tiny MCX, MMCX and U.FL types inside equipment and on modules, and large DIN-type connectors on commercial and repeater cabling for high power.

## What matters in practice

- **Frequency.** The cheaper threaded UHF connector is fine on HF and 2 m. Move to N (or TNC or SMA, if sized for the job) for 70 cm and above.
- **Weather.** None of these stays dry by itself. Wrap outdoor joints with self-amalgamating tape, then electrical tape over it, shaped so water sheds, and form a drip loop in the cable. A wet connector lets water wick into the cable.
- **Installation.** Most failures are in the wiring, not the connector: a cold-soldered shield, a centre pin that is not fully seated, or a crimp that is not tight. Use the right size plug for the cable.
- **Adapters.** Each one adds loss, a possible mismatch and one more joint to fail. Fewer is better.
- **Gender and polarity.** Check before ordering. Some small connectors come in "reverse-polarity" forms (RP-SMA is common on Wi-Fi gear) that look the same but will not mate.
- **Impedance.** Don't mix 50 Ω and 75 Ω versions of the same family: they can mate imperfectly and wear contacts.
