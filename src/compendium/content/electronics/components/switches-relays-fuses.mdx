## What they are

A **switch** opens and closes a circuit by hand. A **relay** does the same electrically: a small current in a coil moves contacts that switch a different, possibly much bigger, circuit. A **fuse** (or circuit breaker) is a deliberate weak point that opens by itself when current is too high. Switches and relays *control*; fuses *protect*.

## Switches

<Switches />

Describe a switch by its **poles** and **throws**: SPST is a plain on/off, SPDT picks between two paths (such as two antennas), DPDT switches two circuits at once. Switches are also **momentary** (spring-return, like a push-to-talk) or **latching**. Check the contact ratings: a switch rated for mains AC can fail quickly on a DC load of similar current, because a DC arc is harder to snuff out.

## Relays

<Relay />

The coil is an electromagnet, so a relay isolates the control circuit from the switched one. That lets a microcontroller key a transmitter, or a front-panel button switch an antenna. Relays come in several forms:

- **General-purpose and power relays** switch DC or AC supplies.
- **RF (coaxial) relays** are built to switch RF with a controlled impedance, for transmit-receive changeover or antenna selection. An ordinary relay is not a substitute at VHF and above.
- **Latching relays** hold their position with no coil current.
- **Solid-state relays** use semiconductors instead of moving contacts: silent and fast, but with a voltage drop and different limits.

A coil stores energy in its magnetic field. When the current is cut, the field collapses and drives the voltage sharply the other way. A **flyback diode** across the coil, with its cathode toward the positive side so it does nothing while the coil is energised, gives that current a path.

## Fuses and breakers

<FuseFire />

A fuse protects the **wiring**, and by extension the equipment and the building. Its rating must be no higher than the current the thinnest wire in the circuit can safely carry; see <Ref to="wire-gauge-and-fusing" /> for the numbers. It is wired in series with the hot (positive) lead, as close as practical to the source, so the whole length of wire is protected.

- A **fast-acting** fuse opens quickly on an overload; a **slow-blow** (time-delay) type tolerates the brief surge when a motor or large capacitor starts.
- A **circuit breaker** does the same job and can be reset. Use ones rated for the voltage and for DC if it is a DC circuit.
- **Replace a blown fuse with the same type and rating** only after finding out why it blew. Never use a larger one, foil or a wire strap.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Fuse the live side">A fuse in the ground or neutral lead leaves the equipment energised when it blows. Put fuses and breakers in the hot conductor. See <Ref to="electrical-safety" />.</Callout>
