## Beyond the call you were given

New licensees receive the next call in sequence from the FCC. Two other routes exist: a **vanity call**, which you choose, and a **special-event call**, a short-lived call for an event. See <Ref to="call-signs" /> for how calls are built.

## Vanity calls

A vanity call is a call sign you request instead of accepting the one assigned. **Any licensed amateur** may request one. People ask for their initials, a short and easy-to-send call (valuable in CW and contests), a call they once held, or a relative's.

How it works in practice:

- You apply to the FCC, normally online, listing choices in order of preference. A fee applies, and the amount can change.
- Which calls you can request depends on your license class. The shortest formats are typically reserved for Amateur Extra operators.
- A call sign released by someone else is typically held back for a waiting period (about two years) before others may request it.
- Once granted, it replaces your old call and goes with you for the life of the license.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Check before you apply">Search a <Ref to="callbooks-and-lookup">callbook</Ref> or the FCC database to see whether the call is free, and read the current FCC rules on eligibility. Some formats and recently held calls are restricted.</Callout>

## Special-event calls

When a club or group operates for a celebration, an anniversary or a public event, it can use a **special-event call sign**: a very short, memorable call, usually a single letter after the number. In the US these are issued for a short period, typically up to about two weeks, through a coordinated volunteer program, and the operators use their own licenses to take part.

Such stations often offer a special QSL card or certificate to those who contact them. See <Ref to="special-events" /> and <Ref to="logging-and-qsl" />.

Other countries run their own versions, often with a special prefix instead of a short suffix. Their rules differ, so check the host country's regulator.

## Identification still applies

Whatever the call, the rules do not change. You still identify at least every 10 minutes and at the end of a contact, using the call sign you operate under. A special-event call is a station call: the control operator must still hold a valid license. See <Ref to="control-operators" />.
