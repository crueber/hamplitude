## A service, not a station

Amateur radio (ham radio) is a licensed radio service that lets ordinary people operate their own transmitters on reserved slices of the spectrum. It exists for **self-training, experimentation and public service**. The word "amateur" means unpaid: you can't be paid to operate, and you can't use it for business.

These aren't just words. They explain why the rules look the way they do: operators are trusted with real power and real spectrum, in return for staying noncommercial and keeping their skills sharp.

## What you can do with it

Almost anything that involves radio:

- talk locally through repeaters, or across the world on HF
- send Morse code, text and pictures, and run digital modes from a laptop
- bounce signals off the Moon, through satellites, or off meteor trails
- build your own antennas and radios
- provide communications when normal systems fail
- compete in contests, chase distant stations, or operate from a mountaintop

See <Ref to="ways-to-get-on-the-air" /> for a fuller map.

## What you can't do

The rules draw a firm line around the hobby:

- no broadcasting to the general public, and no business use
- no payment for operating, and no music
- no secret codes: messages must be in a language others can read, and stations must identify themselves

## How you join

You pass an exam, administered by volunteer examiners, for the class of license you want, and the FCC issues a call sign. See <Ref to="license-classes" /> for what each class allows, and <Ref to="clubs-and-elmers" /> for finding help.
