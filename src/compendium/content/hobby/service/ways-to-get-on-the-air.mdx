## A hobby with many doors

Amateur radio is not one activity but a family of them, and most people settle into two or three. The map below groups what operators actually do. Every entry is explained in its own article, so treat this page as a menu.

## Voice, code and data

Under the activities sit a handful of **modes**, the ways information is carried. Voice is the easiest to start with: <Ref to="fm-voice" /> on VHF and UHF for local contacts, <Ref to="ssb-voice" /> on HF for distance. <Ref to="what-is-cw">CW</Ref> is the oldest and, watt for watt, one of the most effective. Digital modes let a computer do the hard work, so a small station can make contacts a voice signal could not. <Ref to="digital-modes-overview" /> compares them.

## Pushing the limits

Some operators chase the extremes: weak signals bounced off the Moon (<Ref to="eme-moonbounce" />), microwave links over mountain tops (<Ref to="microwaves" />), or <Ref to="amateur-satellites">satellites</Ref> that pass overhead for only a few minutes at a time. If a record exists, someone is probably trying to break it.

## Giving back

Amateur radio's public-service purpose is not a formality. <Ref to="ares-and-races" />, <Ref to="skywarn" /> and <Ref to="public-service-events" /> put the skills to use for neighbours. New to all this? <Ref to="clubs-and-elmers" /> explains how to find people who will help you get started.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Your license sets the menu">Your license class decides which bands you can use, and so which of these doors open first. See <Ref to="license-classes" />. A Technician has full VHF and UHF privileges and limited HF; satellites, repeaters and most local activities are open from day one.</Callout>
