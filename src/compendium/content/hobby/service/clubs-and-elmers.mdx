## Nobody learns this alone

Amateur radio has a long tradition of experienced operators helping newcomers. The informal word for such a helper is an **Elmer**, a mentor who answers questions, lends a hand with an antenna and sits beside you for your first contacts. Clubs, nets and meetings are where Elmers are found.

## Radio clubs

A club is the single most useful thing a new ham can join. Typical benefits:

- **Classes and exam sessions**, often hosted by the club and run by <Ref to="license-classes">volunteer examiners</Ref>
- **Equipment you can borrow or try**, and people who have already made the mistakes you are about to make
- **Repeaters and a club station**, so you can operate before you own a radio
- **Group activities** such as <Ref to="field-day" />, public service events and contests

Clubs can hold a license of their own. A club station license needs a **trustee** and **at least four members**. The trustee does not have to be an Amateur Extra, and the club does not have to belong to a national organisation such as the ARRL. The ARRL is a hobby association, not a regulator.

## Other places to meet people

| Where | What you get |
|---|---|
| <Ref to="nets">Nets</Ref> | Scheduled on-air gatherings; a low-pressure place to practise procedure |
| Hamfests | Swap meets and conventions where gear is bought and sold, with talks and sometimes exam sessions |
| Online groups | Forums, video calls and study groups; handy when no club is nearby |
| Special-interest groups | Groups for <Ref to="summits-on-the-air">SOTA</Ref>, <Ref to="amateur-satellites">satellites</Ref>, <Ref to="what-is-cw">CW</Ref> and more |

## Getting the most from help

- **Ask before you buy.** A mentor can steer you away from gear that does not fit your goals.
- **Listen to a net** before checking in. See <Ref to="operating-etiquette" />.
- **Bring a question, not a shopping list.** "How would you put up an HF antenna in a small yard?" gets better answers than "What radio should I buy?".
- **Pass it on.** Once you know something, you are someone else's Elmer.

<Callout kind="note" title="Opinions vary">Experienced operators disagree about plenty: which antenna, which rig, which mode. A good mentor explains trade-offs. Hear several views before spending money.</Callout>
