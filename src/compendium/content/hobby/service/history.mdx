## Amateurs came first

Radio began as an experimenters' pursuit, and amateurs were there almost from the start. Within a few years of Marconi's first long-distance demonstrations, many hobbyists were building spark-gap transmitters and receivers at home, with few rules about where on the dial they belonged.

## Why licenses appeared

Crowded, unregulated airwaves caused real problems: signals jammed each other, and ships' safety messages competed with hobbyists. Governments responded with licensing. In the US the Radio Act of 1912 required stations and operators to be licensed and moved amateurs onto short wavelengths, then thought to be of little commercial use.

That judgement turned out to be wrong. Amateurs showed in the 1920s that shortwave signals could cross oceans on modest power, and the commercial world followed them there. The international conference of 1927 formalised amateur bands, and the rules have been refined ever since. The pattern repeats: amateurs explore a new frontier, and the results are absorbed by everyone else.

## The service grows up

Two ideas shaped the service we have now. First, amateur radio is a **service** with a public purpose (training, experiment and emergency communication), and that is what justifies the spectrum it gets. See <Ref to="what-is-amateur-radio" />. Second, operators are trusted to regulate themselves, with national bodies such as the FCC setting the framework and international bodies such as the ITU and IARU coordinating between countries.

After the Second World War, new technology kept arriving: single sideband, VHF repeaters, and in 1961 the first amateur satellite, OSCAR 1. Digital modes moved from teleprinters to sound cards, and software now makes contacts possible at signal levels that once needed a giant antenna.

## What has stayed the same

The tools change; the hobby does not. Today's operator still builds antennas, chases distant stations and provides communication when other systems fail. The licensing details change too (the code test is gone, the classes were simplified), which is why the rules are worth reading from a current source. See <Ref to="license-classes" /> and <Ref to="part-97-in-plain-words" />.
