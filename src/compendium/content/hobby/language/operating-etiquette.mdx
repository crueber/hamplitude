## Sharing a band you don't own

Nobody owns a frequency. Except in an emergency, no amateur station has priority over another, so the whole system of manners comes down to one idea: **make room, and make it easy to be heard**. These customs are mostly unwritten, which is why they matter.

## Listen, ask, call

1. **Listen.** Spend long enough to be sure the frequency is clear. You may hear only one side of a contact when the other station is far away, so a frequency that sounds quiet may still be in use.
2. **Ask.** On CW send "QRL?" and your call; on phone say "Is the frequency in use?" and your call. If someone answers, thank them and move on.
3. **Call.** Say "CQ" a few times, "this is", your call sign a few times, then stop and listen. See <Ref to="ham-abbreviations">CQ and friends</Ref> and <Ref to="q-codes" />.

To answer a CQ, send **their call sign, then yours**. Short exchanges first: swap names, locations and a signal report. See <Ref to="signal-reports" />.

## Leaving space

<G2B_Spacing />

Signals take up room: CW needs roughly 150 to 500 Hz, SSB about 2 to 3 kHz. Settle your transmitting frequency that far from your neighbours. Follow the voluntary <Ref to="band-plans">band plan</Ref> for the mode you are using. On HF, the convention is lower sideband below 10 MHz and upper sideband above (60 m is a special case).

## Good habits

- **Identify properly:** at least every 10 minutes during a contact, and at the end. See <Ref to="part-97-in-plain-words" />.
- **Tune up quietly.** Use a dummy load, or listen first, so you do not blast through a QSO. Use only as much power as the contact needs.
- **Take turns.** On a repeater, pause between transmissions so others can join in. Keep transmissions short.
- **Be patient with pile-ups.** Listen to how the DX station works, and do not call over others or when they are in contact. See <Ref to="dx-etiquette-and-split" />.
- **Do not interfere on purpose.** Deliberate interference is prohibited. If someone is jamming, do not answer or argue; it only feeds them.
- **Keep the air clean.** Obscene or indecent language is prohibited.
- **If conditions change** and another station suddenly appears on your frequency, sort it out courteously, in a way acceptable to everyone.
- **If you hear a distress call**, acknowledge it and find out what help is needed. That takes priority over everything.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Everyone was new once">Courtesy extends to newcomers. A clear, patient answer to a nervous first-time operator does more for the hobby than anything else. See <Ref to="clubs-and-elmers" />.</Callout>
