## Rules instead of a license

<Callout kind="note" title="MURS is not amateur radio, and needs no individual license">Part 95 lets eligible people operate MURS without an individual license (95.305), so the rules sit in the **equipment** and in how you use it. The radio must be FCC-certified (95.335), and MURS stations must not be repeaters or signal boosters (95.2733). GMRS needs its own license: <Ref to="gmrs-licensing" />.</Callout>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Not legal advice">This article explains the rules in plain words. You are responsible for operating legally where you live, and the rule text, linked at the end, is the authority.</Callout>

## The limits

| Rule | What it means | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Power | The transmitter's output power must not exceed 2 W | 95.2767 |
| Antenna height | No higher than 60 ft above the ground, or 20 ft above the structure it is mounted on | 95.2741 |
| No relaying | Not a repeater or signal booster, and no store-and-forward packet operation | 95.2733 |
| No phone link | No interconnection with the public switched telephone network | 95.2749 |
| No continuous carrier | The transmitter must not be run in continuous carrier mode | 95.2757 |
| Not in flight | Not authorized aboard aircraft in flight | 95.2707 |
| Certified | The radio must be a certified type, and a MURS radio cannot be certified for both MURS and other subparts (except Part 15) | 95.335, 95.2761 |
| No modifying | Adding frequencies, power or different modulation is not allowed, and it voids certified status | 95.337 |

Antennas must also meet the air-navigation rules in 95.317, which the height rule points to.

## Sharing and interference

MURS channels are shared. Operators must take **reasonable precautions to avoid harmful interference**, including **listening to the frequency for communications in progress before transmitting** (95.2725). There is no channel you can claim.

## What you may not do

MURS has no list of banned topics of its own, but the rule for all Personal Radio Services applies (95.333). In plain words, you may not:

- use the radio for **illegal activity**
- transmit **advertisements or program material tied to broadcasting**
- send **messages for hire** or provide a common-carrier service
- **interfere on purpose** with another station
- use **obscene, profane or indecent language**, or send false communications
