## A shared band that needs no license

<Callout kind="note" title="CB is not amateur radio, and needs no individual license">The CB Radio Service falls under Part 95 of the FCC rules, which lets eligible people operate without an individual license (47 CFR 95.305). That is a different rulebook from <Ref to="what-is-amateur-radio">amateur radio</Ref>. Only FCC-certified CB radios may be used: a ham radio, even one that can tune the CB channels, may not (95.935).</Callout>

The rules define the service as "a mobile and fixed two-way voice communication service for facilitating personal, business or voluntary public service activities, including communications to provide assistance to highway travelers" (95.903). Like <Ref to="what-is-frs" /> and <Ref to="what-is-gmrs" />, it is a **Personal Radio Service**, with its rules built into the radio and into how you use it rather than into a license.

## Forty shared channels

The service has 40 channels, each designated by its center frequency, from 26.965 MHz (channel 1) up to 27.405 MHz (channel 40). No one owns a channel: they are all allotted for shared use.

The numbers are not quite in frequency order. Channel 23 is **27.255**, channel 24 is **27.235** and channel 25 is **27.245**, so the climb in frequency goes 22, 24, 25, 23, 26.

## What you can say, and on which channel

**Channel 9 is for emergencies and traveler assistance only.** It must not be used for anything else (95.931). On every channel, operators must give priority to emergency communications.

Everything else is plain-language two-way voice with other CB stations, for personal, business or volunteer public-service talk. One-way transmissions are allowed only to call for help, warn travelers of hazardous road conditions, make a brief radio check, or page by voice.

## Modes and power at a glance

CB radios transmit AM voice or single sideband voice, and may also transmit FM (95.971). The power ceilings are 4 W for AM or FM and 12 W for SSB; <Ref to="cb-rules-and-limits" /> explains how those are measured and the other limits that go with them.

For the amateur side of the spectrum, see <Ref to="amateur-band-chart" />. CB and amateur radio share neither rules nor equipment.
