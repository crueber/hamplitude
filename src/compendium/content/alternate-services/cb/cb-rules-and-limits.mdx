## Rules instead of a license

<Callout kind="note" title="CB is not amateur radio, and needs no individual license">Part 95 lets eligible people operate CB without an individual license (47 CFR 95.305), so the rules sit in the **equipment** and in how you use it. A ham radio may not be used on the CB channels, even if it can tune them (95.935). See <Ref to="what-is-cb-radio" /> for the band and channels.</Callout>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Not legal advice">This article explains the rules in plain words. You are responsible for operating legally where you live, and the rule text, linked at the end, is the authority.</Callout>

There is no license to lose, but there is still something to lose: **breaking any operating rule in the subpart, the part or the rest of the FCC rules voids your authority to operate a CB station** (95.905).

## The equipment and power

| Rule | What it means | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Power, AM or FM | Mean carrier power (the average power of the carrier) of no more than 4 W | 95.967 |
| Power, SSB | Peak envelope power (the highest power reached on voice peaks) of no more than 12 W | 95.967 |
| No amplifiers | No external RF power amplifier "under any circumstances"; owning one plus other evidence of excess power is presumed use | 95.939 |
| Certified radios | The transmitter must be a certified type, or the user's authority to operate is void | 95.335 |
| No modifying | Adding frequencies, power or different modulation is not allowed and voids certified status | 95.337 |
| Channels only | A CB radio may transmit only on the 40 CB channels, with its frequency controls out of the user's reach | 95.987 |
| Antenna height | No higher than 60 ft above ground, or 20 ft above the building or tree it is mounted on, whichever is higher | 95.941 |

The no-amplifier rule has no exceptions at all, which is why a legal CB station's power is set by the radio's design and not by what you add to it.

## How you may use it

- **Five-minute conversations.** Limit each on-air conversation to five minutes, then stay off that channel for at least one minute. Emergency communications and traveler assistance are exempt (95.957).
- **Channel 9** is for emergencies and traveler assistance only (95.931).
- **One-way transmissions** only to call for help, warn of hazardous road conditions, make brief test transmissions, or page by voice.
- **No advertising** or soliciting the sale of goods or services, and no political candidate advertising (campaign business is allowed).
- **No music, whistling or sound effects** to amuse or to attract attention.
- **No contact with other countries**, except General Radio Service stations in Canada (95.933).
- **No broadcast material** meant for a radio or television station (news gathering is allowed).

Those add to the rules for every Personal Radio Service in 95.333: no illegal activity, no messages for hire, no intentional interference, no obscene or profane language, no false communications.

## Phone and remote links

A CB station may be connected to the public telephone network only by hand, with the operator controlling the station, listening to each conversation, and stopping if a rule is broken (95.949). No one may operate a CB station by wireless remote control, and wired remote control needs the FCC's prior approval (95.945).
