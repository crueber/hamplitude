## Two services, two rulebooks

GMRS and amateur radio both use radios, call signs and repeaters, and both are regulated by the FCC. They are still separate services. Amateur radio runs under Part 97; GMRS runs under Part 95, Subpart E. A license in one gives you **no** privileges in the other, so a ham who wants to use GMRS needs a GMRS license of their own, and the reverse is also true. See <Ref to="gmrs-licensing" /> for how the GMRS license works.

| | GMRS | Amateur radio |
|---|---|---|
| Rules | Part 95, Subpart E | Part 97 |
| To get on the air | an individual license with an application and fee | an exam, then a license: <Ref to="license-classes" /> |
| Who may talk | licensee and immediate family | the licensed operator |
| Minimum age | 18 for a new license | none |
| Identification | call sign at the end and at least every 15 minutes | see <Ref to="call-signs" /> |
| Equipment | certified GMRS radios only | build, modify and use what you like within the rules |
| Content | plain language, no music, no ads | no music or business use; see <Ref to="part-97-in-plain-words" /> |

Everything in the GMRS column comes from the FCC rules, which are listed at the end of this page. For the amateur column, see <Ref to="part-97-in-plain-words" /> and <Ref to="what-is-amateur-radio" />.

## When one license is not enough

The services do not mix. A GMRS station may not send non-emergency messages to an amateur station, Emergency messages are the exception.

The two licenses simply stack. Many hams add a GMRS license for family use: one license that also covers immediate family members, who may operate under it. Operators who hold both keep the rules separate: GMRS identification, GMRS content rules and GMRS equipment on GMRS channels, amateur rules on amateur bands.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Equipment is where hams get caught">An amateur radio that happens to tune GMRS channels is not a certified GMRS radio, and the amateur service lets you modify gear in ways GMRS does not. See <Ref to="gmrs-radios-and-range" />.</Callout>

## Choosing between them

- **GMRS** suits family and group communication with simple radios and a license any adult can apply for. Its limits are a fixed set of channels and certified equipment.
- **Amateur radio** is for learning and experimenting across many bands, modes and power levels, in return for passing an exam.

Neither replaces the other, and you can use both.
