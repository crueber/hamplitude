## One license for the household

<Callout kind="note" title="A license of its own, not an amateur privilege">GMRS is licensed under **Part 95, Subpart E**, not Part 97. It is a separate license with a separate call sign. Holding a ham license gives you no GMRS privileges, and a GMRS license gives you none on amateur bands. See <Ref to="gmrs-and-ham-radio" />.</Callout>

A valid **individual license** is required to operate a GMRS station, and the applicant pays the application and regulatory fees (47 CFR 95.1705). The rules ask for an application and fees; they contain no exam. A new license is open only to someone at least 18 years old. Licenses normally run **ten years** and can be renewed.

## Who may transmit

The license belongs to one person, but it covers more than that person (95.1705(c)):

- **the licensee**, on the licensee's own stations
- **immediate family**: spouse, children, grandchildren, stepchildren, parents, grandparents, stepparents, brothers, sisters, aunts, uncles, nieces, nephews and in-laws
- **anyone at all**, but only to send an emergency message

The licensee decides which family members may operate and remains responsible at all times for proper operation. Operators under 18 are not personally responsible for improper operation of a repeater or base station; the licensee is (95.1743).

## Repeaters and sharing

A licensee may let anyone use their GMRS repeater, may limit it to chosen people, and may bar specific people (95.1705(d)). See <Ref to="gmrs-repeaters" />.

## Identifying yourself

Each GMRS station identifies with its FCC-assigned call sign at the end of transmissions and at least every 15 minutes during a long series, in English voice or in Morse code sent as an audible tone (95.1751). A unit number may follow the call sign. A GMRS call sign is not an amateur call sign (see <Ref to="call-signs" />), and each is used only in its own service. A repeater need not identify if it only retransmits stations of its own license that identify properly.
