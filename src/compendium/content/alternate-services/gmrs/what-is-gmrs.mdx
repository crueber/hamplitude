## A radio service of its own

The **General Mobile Radio Service (GMRS)** is a two-way voice radio service, with limited data, "for facilitating activities of individual licensees and their family members", including voluntary help to the public in emergencies (47 CFR 95.1703). People use it to keep a family, a convoy or a hiking group in touch. It works on UHF channels near 462 and 467 MHz and is governed by Part 95, the rules for the **Personal Radio Services**, not by Part 97.

<Callout kind="note" title="GMRS is not amateur radio">GMRS has its **own licensing structure**: an individual GMRS license under Part 95, Subpart E, with its own call sign and its own rules. An amateur license does not let you transmit on GMRS channels, and a GMRS license does not let you use amateur frequencies. You can hold both. See <Ref to="gmrs-licensing" />.</Callout>

## GMRS, FRS and amateur radio side by side

The **Family Radio Service (FRS)** is the license-free cousin. It is the service behind the inexpensive handhelds sold in pairs. All 22 FRS channels are also allotted to GMRS, so the two services share spectrum and can talk to each other (95.563, 95.1731). The difference is the license, the power and the equipment.

| | FRS | GMRS | Amateur |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rulebook | Part 95, Subpart B | Part 95, Subpart E | Part 97 |
| Individual license | none | required | required |
| Exam | none | none in the rules | required |
| Who may transmit | any eligible person | licensee and immediate family | licensed operators |
| Equipment | certified; antenna fixed to the radio | certified | no certification required |
| Channels | 22, shared with GMRS | 30, including the FRS 22 | amateur bands |

## What you may and may not do

On GMRS you hold plain-language voice conversations with other GMRS stations and with FRS units, about personal or business activities (95.1731). Any channel may be used for emergency communications, and emergencies always take priority.

The rules are narrower than amateur radio in several ways (95.1733):

- no music or entertainment, no advertising, no coded messages ("10 codes" are fine)
- no continuous transmissions, except for the immediate safety of life or property
- no messages to amateur stations, except in an emergency

And because every GMRS transmitter must be FCC-certified, you cannot build or modify your own as an amateur may. The next articles cover <Ref to="gmrs-licensing" />, <Ref to="gmrs-channels-and-power" /> and <Ref to="gmrs-radios-and-range" />.
