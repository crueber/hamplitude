## What a GMRS repeater does

<Callout kind="note" title="Not amateur radio">
A GMRS repeater runs under a GMRS license, not an amateur one. Using it takes a GMRS license of your own, or coverage as a licensee's immediate family; see <Ref to="gmrs-licensing" />.
</Callout>

A repeater listens on one frequency and retransmits what it hears on another, from a good site, so that two radios too far apart to hear each other can both reach it. The idea is the one in <Ref to="repeaters" />, and the same range limits apply (see <Ref to="line-of-sight" />). On GMRS it uses the eight main channels near 462 MHz.

## The eight pairs

The repeater's **output** is one of the 462 MHz main channels, and its **input** is the matching 467 MHz main channel, exactly 5 MHz higher. Your radio transmits on the input and listens on the output.

<GmrsRepeaters_Pairs />

Handhelds, mobiles and control stations may use the 467 MHz main channels **only to talk through a repeater** (or for a brief test). They are not simplex channels. The offset is the same 5 MHz that is common on 70 cm amateur repeaters, covered in <Ref to="uhf-repeaters" />.

## Tones and access

Radios open a repeater with an audible or subaudible tone, the kind of access tone described in <Ref to="squelch-and-tones" />. The GMRS rules allow tones to access repeater stations. The channels are shared and are not assigned to any one repeater (47 CFR 95.359), so operators must cooperate to avoid interference. If they cannot settle a problem themselves, the FCC may restrict channels, power, antenna height, area or hours.

## Who may use it

The licensee who owns a repeater chooses who uses it. The rules let the licensee allow **any person** to use the repeater, or limit it to specific people, and bar particular people when needed. Some repeaters are open to all, some are private to a family or a group, and the licensee decides which.

Identification uses the licensee's FCC-assigned call sign. A repeater need not identify if it retransmits only stations under its own license and those stations identify properly. Otherwise it must, at the end of transmissions and at least every 15 minutes during a long one, by voice in English or by Morse tone.

## Control, and what is not allowed

A GMRS repeater may be run by automatic control, and the repeater, base and fixed stations may be remote controlled. Connecting a station to a telephone line is prohibited; a repeater, base or fixed station may be tied to a network only for the sole purpose of that remote control. So a GMRS repeater is not a phone patch.
