## What LoRa is

LoRa is the radio technique under Meshtastic and similar meshes. It uses **chirp spread spectrum**: a chirp is a signal that sweeps in frequency across the channel, upward or downward, and each symbol is a chirp shifted by some amount. It belongs to the family covered in <Ref to="spread-spectrum" />. A transmission begins with a preamble of chirps that lets receivers find and time it, and then carries the packet. Radios on the same mesh must agree on the same frequency, bandwidth and spreading factor.

Three settings decide what a link can do:

- **Spreading factor (SF)**: how much each symbol is stretched in time. Each step up **doubles the airtime** and adds about **2.5 dB** of link budget.
- **Bandwidth**: how wide a slice of spectrum is used. Each doubling costs almost **3 dB** of link budget.
- **Coding rate**: how much redundancy resists noise. Higher rates are more reliable and slower. The overhead is 1.25x at 4/5, 1.5x at 4/6, 1.75x at 4/7 and 2x at 4/8.

## Range against speed

**Link budget** is how much loss a link can absorb and still be received; see <Ref to="decibels" /> and <Ref to="noise-and-snr" />. A bigger budget means more range, and the price is a slower data rate. The Meshtastic project groups settings into **modem presets**, and its documentation lists these figures for the nine general-purpose ones.

<LoraForMeshes_Tradeoff />

| Meshtastic preset | SF | Bandwidth | Data rate | Link budget |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Short Fast | 7 | 250 kHz | 10.94 kbps | 143 dB |
| Medium Slow | 10 | 250 kHz | 1.95 kbps | 150.5 dB |
| Long Fast | 11 | 250 kHz | 1.07 kbps | 153 dB |
| Long Moderate | 11 | 125 kHz | 0.34 kbps | 156 dB |

The documentation's link budgets assume a transmit power of 22 dBm and an antenna with 0 dB gain, and the data rates are theoretical maximums that ignore headers, hops and retransmissions.

## What it means for a mesh

Even the fastest preset tops out near 22 kbps, and the three Long presets listed carry between 0.34 and 1.34 kbps, so a LoRa mesh is built for short text and position reports, not for pictures or video (compare <Ref to="aredn-mesh" />). Slower settings also keep a transmitter on the air longer per message, which matters when many nodes share a channel (see <Ref to="what-is-a-manet" />).

LoRa radios commonly use license-free sub-GHz bands that differ by region. The rules for those bands, and what changes if someone operates on amateur frequencies, are in <Ref to="meshtastic-and-the-rules" />.
