## What it is

Marathons, bike rides, parades and charity walks cover too much ground for the organisers' eyes alone. Amateur operators provide the **communications layer**: a radio at each aid station and checkpoint, so that medical needs, lost participants, supply shortages and course problems reach the people who can deal with them. These events are good practice for emergency communication, because the pressure is real but the stakes are lower.

## How it is organised

<PublicServiceEvents_Course />

A **net control station** at event headquarters runs the net, as described in <Ref to="net-control" />. Operators at fixed points (start, aid stations, turnaround, finish) report what they see. A **mobile or sweep** operator travels with the last participant or follows the course, and others may ride with medical or support vehicles. Everyone reports to net control; net control decides what to do next and talks to the organisers.

Typical things operators report:

- **Medical needs**: where, how many, how urgent.
- **Participants** who need transport, are lost or have dropped out.
- **Supplies** running low at an aid station.
- **Progress**: lead and last participants passing each point.

## Before the day

- **Meet the organiser.** Agree what they want from the operators, who is in charge, and who to call for what.
- **Choose frequencies** with the net control operator. Simplex, or a local repeater with the owner's permission; plan a backup.
- **Walk or drive the course** to find radio dead spots and note the locations.
- **Prepare a list of locations** with unambiguous names, so reports are precise. "Mile 14, water stop on the left" beats "near the corner".

## Rules to remember

- **You cannot be paid** to operate. These are volunteer jobs; see <Ref to="part-97-in-plain-words" /> for the details. If you are unsure whether a specific arrangement is allowed, ask.
- **Identify** with your call sign as usual; a net does not change that.
- **Keep it plain.** Use plain language and describe exactly what you see, rather than slang.
- **Stay in your role**: report, do not decide. Medical and safety decisions belong to the people trained for them.

## In practice

Bring your <Ref to="go-kits">go kit</Ref>, a printed course map, water, sun and weather protection, and a spare battery. A high-visibility vest is common. Sit where you can see participants but are out of the way of them and traffic. Many groups use these events as a regular chance to train.
