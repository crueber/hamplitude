## What it is

**CERT**, the Community Emergency Response Team program, trains ordinary residents in basic disaster response so they can help their neighbours in the first hours of a disaster, before professional responders can reach everyone. It is a local program, typically run through a fire department or emergency management office. CERT is mainly about first aid, fire safety and rescue rather than radio, and amateur radio is mainly about communication, which is exactly why the two fit together well.

## How hams fit

<CertAndCommunityEvents_Venn />

A CERT team that can't talk to its coordinator, or to other teams, is limited to what it can reach on foot. Amateurs bring skills teams often lack: running a net, getting a signal out of a damaged area, powering a radio from a battery, and passing a message exactly. Some amateurs take the CERT course themselves and serve as both, which is the most useful combination. Teams may have simple handheld radios as well, and a ham's job is to extend and organise that communication rather than replace it.

Whatever you do, you remain an amateur under the FCC rules: no payment for operating, and the same identification and operating rules as at any other time. How a team uses radios in a real response depends on the local agency, so work with them in advance, not for the first time during a crisis.

## Community events

Charity walks and rides, marathons, parades and fairs spread people over a large area, where phones are often congested. Amateurs provide **communication between checkpoints and the organisers**: reporting where help is needed, tracking participants, relaying a lost-child report. The volunteer is the radio link, not the security or medical staff. See <Ref to="public-service-events" />.

These events also double as practice. A busy event is a chance to rehearse net discipline, short and clear messages, working from a go-kit and a portable antenna, and operating for hours on battery, all while the stakes are low. Field Day plays a similar role; see <Ref to="field-day" />.

## Getting involved

- Ask your local fire department or emergency management office about CERT classes. Many communities run them regularly.
- Ask your local club about public-service events, who organises them and what the commitment is.
- Join a net or two first. Radio skill is built on regular practice; see <Ref to="nets" />.
- Programs and requirements are set locally, so ask your local group.
