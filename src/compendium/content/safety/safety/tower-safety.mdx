## What it is

Raising an antenna means working at height, with heavy parts, near overhead wires, and often near live equipment. The serious hazards are **falls** and **power-line contact**, and both are fatal in an instant. Preparation and a willingness to stop matter more than any single piece of gear.

## Falls

<Climb />

Climb only when **trained**, with an approved, **fall-arrest** harness rated for your weight and still within its service life, inspected before every use. A waist belt for positioning is not fall protection. Use **100% tie-off**: at least one lanyard is on the tower whenever you are above the ground, and you clip the second before you unclip the first.

Attach to the **tower legs**, which are strong structural members, never to a rung, an antenna mount, or a cable. A shock-absorbing lanyard anchored **above head level** keeps a fall short; anchored low, it lets you fall farther and hit harder.

Always have a **helper on the ground** who can call for help, and a plan to get you down. A person hanging motionless in a harness can deteriorate within minutes.

**Crank-up and tilt-over towers** have specific rules: follow the manufacturer's instructions, and never climb one that is extended without its mechanical safety locks installed.

## Overhead wires

<PowerLineClear />

Look up before anything is raised, and map where every part could fall: while you raise it, while it stands, and if a guy, a bolt or the structure fails. **No part may come within 10 feet of a power line** on the way down. Treat 10 feet as a bare minimum: higher-voltage lines call for more, and the utility can advise. Voltage can arc across a gap, so "close" is enough. Rope, an aluminum mast and a metal ladder can all conduct, and wet ones especially.

Never attach an antenna to a utility pole. If the site has wires you cannot safely avoid, choose another site or call the utility.

## Before you go up

<TowerSafety_GoNoGo />

- **Lock out and tag** every circuit that feeds the tower, such as rotators, amplifiers and lighting. Switch off transmitters, since the strongest fields are near the antenna. Do not unground the tower.
- Keep everyone out of the drop zone, wear a hard hat, and tie off tools or carry them in a bucket or bag.
- Never climb in wind, rain, ice, darkness or when thunderstorms are forecast. Wet metal is slippery and conducts.
- Check guy anchors, turnbuckles and hardware. A **safety wire** through a turnbuckle stops vibration from loosening it.

<Callout kind="warn" title="When to hire a professional">Tall towers, anything near power lines, heavy beams, crane work and unfamiliar structures belong to a professional tower crew. No antenna is worth a life. Check local building codes and permits before erecting a tower.</Callout>
