## What it is

A **callbook** is a directory of licensed operators, keyed by call sign. It answers "who is that?" in a second: name, location, license class, and often more. Once printed in thick volumes, callbooks are now searched online or built into logging software. Nothing in them is secret: license records are public.

## Sources

<CallbooksAndLookup_Sources />

- **The official licensing database.** In the US, the FCC keeps the record of every license: holder, class, call sign, grant and expiry dates, and the address on file. It is the authority on whether a call sign is valid and current. Other countries keep similar registers; their detail and access differ.
- **Community callbook sites.** Operators add a biography, photo, QSL instructions and other details. They often copy license data, so they can lag behind it.
- **Logging software.** It queries one or more of these sources as you type a call sign, and fills in name, location and grid square for you. See <Ref to="logging-software" />.

Some uses need **no lookup at all**: the prefix of a call sign tells you the country, and in the US the number usually tells you the region. See <Ref to="call-signs" />.

## What to do with it

- **Name and location** for a friendlier QSO, or to check your log.
- **Grid square** and distance for contests and awards. See <Ref to="logging-and-qsl" />.
- **QSL information**: whether and how the operator confirms contacts.
- **Is this call sign valid?** An unfamiliar call that appears nowhere may be a mistake, a very new license or a station that should not be on the air.
- **Prefix lookups** for country credit in DX awards.

## Cautions

- **Information goes stale.** People move, change class or let a license lapse, so a callbook shows what was on file, not where the operator is now. A portable station is not at its home address.
- **Self-reported data is unverified.** A bio is what the person wrote.
- **Privacy.** The address you give the licensing authority becomes part of a public record. Think about which address you provide; many operators use a mailing address (such as a post office box) where that is allowed.
- **Be courteous.** Looking up a call sign is normal. Using what you find to turn up uninvited is not, nor is publishing someone's details.
- Check each service's own policy: what is shown, what is free and how to correct your record changes over time.
