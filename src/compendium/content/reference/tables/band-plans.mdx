## Rules and plans

Every amateur band has two layers. The FCC's rules are mandatory: they fix the band edges, power limits and which emissions each part of the band may carry. On top of that, operators agree a **band plan**: a voluntary guideline for using different modes or activities within a band. Breaking a band plan is not an FCC violation, but following it keeps SSB out of the digital segment and weak CW signals from being drowned out, so everybody gets more contacts.

## Why the plans look the way they do

- **Narrow modes go low.** CW and most digital modes are only a few hundred hertz wide or less, so they cluster together, away from wide voice signals that would otherwise swamp them.
- **Weak-signal work needs quiet.** The DX windows and weak-signal segments exist so that faint signals are not covered by repeaters or nearby stations.
- **Repeaters need coordination.** FM repeaters are paired inputs and outputs, so their channels are laid out in blocks. Local volunteer groups called frequency coordinators assign them. See <Ref to="repeaters" />.
- **Calling frequencies bring people together.** Everyone agrees where to meet, then moves off to talk. The national FM simplex calling frequency on 2 m is 146.520 MHz.
- **Digital modes cluster on shared frequencies.** Weak-signal modes such as FT8 use agreed dial frequencies on each band so that stations can find each other. See <Ref to="ft8-and-ft4" />.

## Real examples

| Where | What it is | Rule or plan? |
|---|---|---|
| 6 m, 50.0 to 50.1 MHz | CW only | FCC rule |
| 6 m, 50.1 to 50.125 MHz | DX window: contacts with stations outside the contiguous 48 states | Voluntary plan |
| 2 m, 144.0 to 144.1 MHz | CW only | FCC rule |
| 2 m, 146.52 MHz | National FM simplex calling frequency | Voluntary plan |
| 2 m repeaters | Commonly offset by plus or minus 600 kHz | Local coordination |
| 70 cm repeaters | Commonly offset by plus or minus 5 MHz | Local coordination |
| 30 m | No phone, no images | FCC rule |
| HF, below 10 MHz | Lower sideband for voice (60 m uses upper) | Convention |
| HF, above 10 MHz | Upper sideband for voice | Convention |

These examples are only illustrative: band plans vary with region and change over time. Look for your national society's plan (the ARRL publishes one for the US) and your local repeater coordinator's listings.
