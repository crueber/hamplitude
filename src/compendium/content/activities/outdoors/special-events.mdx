## What it is

Besides the everyday bands, there is a calendar of **temporary operations**: stations that appear for a day, a weekend or a month, to mark an event or to activate a place that has no permanent amateur. Chasing them, or running one, is a good way to collect contacts you could not make from a normal station.

## Three programs

<SpecialEvents_Programs />

**Special event stations** celebrate an anniversary, a festival, a milestone or an organisation. In the US they often use a special event call sign, which is a short-term call sign available for such events; see <Ref to="vanity-and-special-event-calls" />. Operators usually offer a certificate for those who contact them, and some publish a way to download or request it.

**IOTA** groups the world's islands into numbered **groups**, each identified by a continent code and a number. An operator on an island (or a chaser contacting one) records the group. Awards go to those who collect a range of groups. Islands that are hard to reach, from remote coasts and sandbars to far-flung territories, are favourite expedition targets.

**Lighthouse programs** list lighthouses (and in some cases lightships and other structures) and encourage operators to contact them from or near the structure. Several organisations run these programs, and a weekend each year draws many activators; check what each one currently offers.

## How you take part

- **As a chaser,** watch for announcements and spots. Work the station, log it exactly and, if you want a certificate, follow the instructions to claim it. A special call sign must be copied correctly; ask for it to be repeated.
- **As an activator,** pick a place, check any permission or access requirement, set up a portable station (see <Ref to="portable-field-antennas" />) and announce your operation in the usual places.
- **Run a club event.** A special event station is a good way for a club to attract visitors and new members.

## Things to check

- **Confirmation.** Special event contacts can often be confirmed through the same systems as ordinary ones, such as Logbook of The World. Check each program's own rules for what it accepts. See <Ref to="logging-and-qsl" />.
- **Call sign use.** An operator must still be licensed, must identify properly and must stay within the license class. A special event call sign does not change those limits.
- **Fake claims.** Look for a program's official list or a trusted announcement. Only a station that actually appears in the program's listing is part of it.
- **Timing.** Many such stations are on for a short period. Plan your chase around the band's openings; see <Ref to="skywave-and-skip" />.
