## What it is

Summits on the Air (SOTA) is an award program in which operators climb listed mountain summits, set up a small station on top and make contacts. The climber is the **activator**; the stations who answer, often from home, are **chasers**. The radio is the lightest part of the load: the exercise, navigation and weather are the main event.

## Why a summit

<SummitsOnTheAir_Profile />

Height does two things. For VHF and UHF it extends **line of sight** over lowland, so even a handheld with a small antenna can reach a long way (see <Ref to="line-of-sight" />). For HF, a clear summit has little around it to block or absorb the signal, and a low take-off angle helps with distance (see <Ref to="ground-and-height" />). The program lists each summit by a reference, and points typically depend on the summit's height. Details change: check the current rules.

## The station

- **Radio.** A handheld for FM on VHF, or a lightweight HF transceiver. Low power (QRP) is common; see <Ref to="qrp-radios" />.
- **Antenna.** A light wire (a dipole or end-fed half wave) hung from a pole or a tree, or a small vertical. See <Ref to="portable-field-antennas" /> and <Ref to="end-fed-half-wave" />.
- **Power.** A small lithium or similar battery pack; see <Ref to="portable-power" />.
- **Key, mic or digital.** CW is popular because the gear weighs so little, but voice works too.
- **Logging.** A notebook and pencil that works in the cold and rain, or an app, then upload later.
- **Spotting.** If you have cell coverage, post a spot; if not, announce on a calling frequency, and say your reference.

## Safety first

This is the most important part of the article. A summit is a mountain, and radio is a distraction from it.

- **Tell someone** where you are going and when you will be back.
- **Check the forecast** and turn back early. Thunderstorms and lightning on an exposed summit are deadly. See <Ref to="lightning-safety" />.
- **Carry what you need to survive** a night out if something goes wrong: layers, food, water, a light, navigation and a first-aid kit.
- **Plan to be off the mountain before dark.** Allow time for a tired, careful descent, and carry a light.
- **Know your limits** and your route. The contact is never worth the risk.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Do not stay for a contact in bad weather">If clouds build or thunder sounds, pack up and descend at once. Lightning does not need rain to strike a summit.</Callout>
