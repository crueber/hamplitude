## What it is

A net is a scheduled, organised on-air meeting where many stations share one frequency under a **net control station** (NCS), who keeps order and decides who speaks. Nets exist for everything from weekly club chatter to emergency drills, and checking into one is one of the easiest ways to make your first contacts.

## Kinds of net

<Nets_Kinds />

The category matters less than the habits. A **social** net is largely a ragchew with a host. A **traffic** net passes formal written messages (see <Ref to="radiograms" />). An **emergency** net practises, or carries, communications for a community (see <Ref to="net-control" />). Most nets are on the air at the same time every week, so you can plan around them.

## Finding and joining one

- **Ask your club.** Most clubs run at least one net on a local repeater; see <Ref to="clubs-and-elmers" />.
- **Check repeater directories and club websites** for net listings and schedules (see <Ref to="repeater-directories" />).
- **Listen first.** One session of listening shows you the net's name, who controls it, and how check-ins work.
- **Check in with your call sign when invited**, then wait. The NCS will ask for anything else.
- **Check out** when you leave so net control is not left calling you.

The procedure itself (open, check-in, business, close) is in <Ref to="voice-nets" />. Directed nets, where you transmit only when net control tells you to, are common for large groups; informal nets are more relaxed.

## Why bother

- **Practice.** A net is a friendly place to learn microphone technique and call-sign habits.
- **Community.** You hear the same voices week after week and make real contacts.
- **Readiness.** Emergency and public-service nets rely on operators who already know the procedure.
- **Information.** Club news, repeater changes and weather notices travel by net.

<Callout kind="note" title="Nets do not own the frequency">No amateur station has priority on a frequency except in an emergency. If a net meets on a busy frequency, check first, and expect the rest of us to move politely if asked.</Callout>
