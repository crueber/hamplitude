## What it is

In amateur radio, **DX** means a distant station, often one in another country. DXing is the pursuit of such contacts: tuning for faraway signals, working them, and collecting confirmations. It rewards patience, good listening and a feel for <Ref to="skywave-and-skip">propagation</Ref> more than raw power.

## The loop

<Dxing_Chase />

Stations are often counted as **entities**, which are not quite the same as sovereign countries. A remote island or territory can be its own entity even when it belongs to a larger nation. Awards such as DXCC count entities; see <Ref to="awards" />. Check the program's current list.

## What helps

- **A decent antenna.** Even a simple wire at a good height, working at a low take-off angle, will beat a lot of power on a poor one. A beam is better still. See <Ref to="ground-and-height" />.
- **Knowing the bands.** Higher HF bands are good in daylight when conditions are right; lower bands carry long paths at night. The <Ref to="grey-line-and-long-path">grey line</Ref> around dawn and dusk can open unusual paths.
- **A way to find activity.** DX clusters and the Reverse Beacon Network post stations as they are heard. See <Ref to="spotting-networks" />.
- **Good receive.** In DXing you hear many stations you cannot work. A quiet location and receiver, and a <Ref to="noise-cancelling">noise-reducing</Ref> setup, matter as much as transmit power.
- **A willingness to wait.** Rare stations may be on the air for only days. Expeditions and special events can draw large crowds; see <Ref to="dx-etiquette-and-split" />.

## Calling CQ DX

Two small rules of manners apply on voice:

<G2A_Calling />

A US station calling "CQ DX" is asking for stations outside the contiguous 48 states; answer only if you are one. If you are in the lower 48, you wait for a DX station to call CQ, or you call it directly.

<Callout kind="tip" title="Listen more than you call">A DX operator who is working 100 stations an hour does not want your signal report and name. Listen, find out how they work, then give them the briefest possible call.</Callout>
