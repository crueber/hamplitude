## What it is

Radio direction finding (RDF) means working out where a signal is coming from by measuring the direction it arrives from. One reading gives a direction, not a place; the skill is combining several. Amateurs use it for fox hunts, for tracking down interference, and for finding a stuck transmitter or a lost beacon. The antennas that make it possible are in <Ref to="direction-finding-antennas" />; this article is about the technique.

## Bearings and a fix

<T8C_Bearings />

A bearing is a line on the map, not a point. Take one, move to a different place, take another, and the lines cross at the transmitter. A third bearing from a third place tells you whether to trust the first two: if all three agree, you have it.

Real bearings are never exact, so each is better thought of as a **wedge** whose width grows with distance: an error of 5° is about 90 m sideways at 1 km. Where the wedges overlap is where the transmitter might be.

<RdfBasics_Fix />

Two lessons fall out of this. A smaller reading error shrinks the overlap proportionally. And the **angle between the bearings** matters as much as their accuracy. With a 5° error, a 90° crossing leaves an uncertain region about a quarter of your distance across; at 30° it is more than half, a long thin smear. Bearings taken from nearly the same direction say almost nothing about distance. So move sideways, not forwards.

## Taking a good reading

- **Stand clear.** Hold the antenna out at arm's length. Your body, a vehicle or a metal fence close to it distorts the pattern and bends the bearing.
- **Match polarization.** Hold the antenna so it matches the transmitter's polarization, usually vertical on VHF. A mismatch weakens the signal and can blur the bearing; see <Ref to="polarization" />.
- **Read from open, high ground** where you can. Buildings, trees and hills reflect and block signals.
- **Take more than one reading** at each place, turning the antenna through the whole circle. If the signal seems to come from several directions, believe the strongest or most repeatable.
- **Agree on north.** A compass points to magnetic north, while many maps are drawn to true north. The difference, called declination, depends on where you are. Correct for it, or use one reference throughout.

## When bearings lie

| Problem | What you see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Reflections (multipath) | Bearing changes when you move a few steps | Take several readings; trust the ones that agree |
| Front-or-back ambiguity | Two opposite directions both look right | Use a sense antenna, or take a second bearing from elsewhere |
| Receiver overload | Strong signal from every direction | Add attenuation until the meter moves again |
| Sky-wave on HF | Direction wanders, bearing makes little sense | Hunt on VHF or UHF where the signal travels in a straight line |

Near the transmitter, a bearing from one spot is no longer enough. Switch from plotting lines to simply walking toward the strongest signal, reducing sensitivity as you go; <Ref to="fox-hunting-and-ardf" /> covers that final approach.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Safety">Watch where you are walking and driving, not at the meter. Stay out of private property and away from roads, traffic and power lines, and never climb to reach a transmitter.</Callout>
