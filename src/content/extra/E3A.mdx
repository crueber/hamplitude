<Concept id="eme" title="EME: both stations just need to see the Moon" qs="E3A01 E3A02 E3A03">

**Earth-Moon-Earth** bounces your signal off the Moon. Any two stations with the Moon above both horizons can work each other, up to about **12,000 miles** apart. The Moon's rocking (**libration**) gives **fluttery, irregular fading**.

<Eme />

The Moon is nearest at **perigee**: the shortest trip, so the **least path loss**.

<Key>Perigee, least loss. Both stations must see the Moon.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="em-wave" title="E, H and travel are mutually perpendicular; the medium sets the speed" qs="E3A04 E3A05 E3A10 E3A14">

<EmWave />

The electric and magnetic fields are at **right angles** to each other, and the wave travels at a right angle to both. How fast it goes in a medium is set by the **index of refraction**.

<Index />

In a **circularly polarized** wave the electric and magnetic fields **rotate** as the wave travels.

<Circular />

<Key>Fields at right angles. Speed = c ÷ index of refraction. Circular: rotating fields.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="meteor" title="Meteor scatter: brief trails in the E region, 28 to 148 MHz" qs="E3A08 E3A09">

A meteor burning up leaves a thin ionized column in the **E region**. It briefly reflects signals, so contacts arrive in short bursts. The best range is **28 – 148 MHz**.

<Meteor />

<Key>Meteor trails: E region. Best 28 – 148 MHz.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="aurora" title="Aurora: severe storms, and CW works best" qs="E3A12 E3A13">

<Aurora />

**Severe geomagnetic storms** push the aurora toward the equator, where it can scatter signals. The reflection garbles voice, so **CW** is the best mode.

<Key>Aurora needs a severe geomagnetic storm. Use CW.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="duct" title="Microwave ducts form over large bodies of water" qs="E3A07 E3A11">

<WaterDuct />

A layer of air over water traps microwaves. Typical range is **100 to 300 miles**.

<Key>Over water, 100 – 300 miles.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="muf-night" title="When darkness lowers the MUF, move to a lower band" qs="E3A06">

<DailyMuf />

The maximum usable frequency (**MUF**) falls after dark. When it drops below your frequency, the signal is no longer refracted back. Switch to a **lower frequency band**.

<Key>MUF falls at night: go lower.</Key>

</Concept>
