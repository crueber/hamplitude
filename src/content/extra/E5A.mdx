<Concept id="f0" title="Resonance is the frequency where XL equals XC" qs="E5A02 E5A10">

XL grows with frequency and XC shrinks, so there is exactly one frequency where they are equal. Solve XL = XC for f and you get the resonant frequency. **R does not change it.**

<Formula label="Resonant frequency">f₀ = 1 ÷ (2π × √(L × C))</Formula>

<E5A_ResonantFreq />

| L | C | √(L×C) | f₀ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50 µH | 40 pF | 4.472 × 10⁻⁸ | 1 ÷ (2π × 4.472 × 10⁻⁸) = **3.56 MHz** |
| 50 µH | 10 pF | 2.236 × 10⁻⁸ | 1 ÷ (2π × 2.236 × 10⁻⁸) = **7.12 MHz** |

<Key>4× the capacitance (or inductance) halves f₀. Use henries and farads, then µ = 10⁻⁶ and p = 10⁻¹².</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="series-parallel" title="At resonance, series Z dips and parallel Z peaks" qs="E5A03 E5A04 E5A06 E5A07 E5A08">

At f₀ the inductor and capacitor effects cancel, leaving only **R**. Voltage and current are **in phase**, because there is no net reactance to shift them.

<E5A_SeriesParallelZ />

| At resonance | Series RLC | Parallel RLC |
|---|---|---|
| Impedance | **minimum**, about R | **maximum**, about R |
| Current from the source | **maximum** | **minimum** |
| Current circulating between L and C | n/a | **maximum** |

<Mnemonic>Series is the easy path: low Z, big current. Parallel is a roadblock: high Z, little line current, yet a big current swings round inside the loop.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="q" title="Higher Q means a narrower, taller response" qs="E5A01 E5A05 E5A09 E5A11 E5A12 E5A13">

**Q** measures how little a circuit wastes per cycle. Half-power bandwidth is the width between the points where power has fallen to half.

<Formula label="Bandwidth">BW = f₀ ÷ Q</Formula>

<E5A_QBandwidth />

- **Series**: Q = X ÷ R. **Parallel**: Q = R ÷ X.
- Higher Q: **narrower bandwidth**, including in an impedance-matching circuit.
- Series circuit: voltage across L or C is **Q times** the source voltage, so it can exceed it. Higher Q raises these internal voltages.

<Key>BW = f₀ ÷ Q: 7.1 MHz ÷ 150 = 47.3 kHz. Series Q is X over R, parallel Q is R over X.</Key>

</Concept>
