<Concept id="control" title="Unattended stations: limited traffic, 3 minutes to shut down" qs="E1C05 E1C08">

<E1C_LinkLoss />

* **Remote control:** if the control link malfunctions, the station must stop transmitting within **3 minutes**.
* **Automatic control:** third-party traffic only with **RTTY or data**, never voice or CW.

<Key>Link lost: off in 3 minutes. Automatic third party: data only.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="lowbands" title="630 m and 2200 m: tell the UTC, wait 30 days" qs="E1C03 E1C07 E1C12">

<E1C_UtcFlow />

Power-line carrier (PLC) systems share these frequencies, so you notify the **Utilities Technology Council** with your **call sign and coordinates**. No FCC endorsement, no FAA filing.

</Concept>

<Concept id="purity" title="Three signal limits to know" qs="E1C01 E1C09 E1C10">

<E1C_Spur />

| Rule | Limit |
|---|---|
| Data on **60 m** | **2.8 kHz** maximum bandwidth |
| Angle modulation **below 29.0 MHz** | modulation index no higher than **1.0** at the highest modulating frequency |
| Spurious below **30 MHz** | **-43 dB** relative to the fundamental |

<Mnemonic>Below 30 MHz: spurs at least 43 dB down. A bigger negative number is weaker.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="abroad" title="Operating abroad: CEPT, IARP, and what you may say" qs="E1C02 E1C04 E1C06 E1C11">

<E1C_Abroad />

**CEPT** works both ways between the US and many European countries. You carry a copy of **FCC Public Notice DA 16-1048**. **IARP** is the equivalent permit for certain countries in the **Americas**.

</Concept>
