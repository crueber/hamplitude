<Concept id="opamp-what" title="An op-amp: huge gain, hardly loads the input, drives the output hard" qs="E7G01 E7G03 E7G12">

An **operational amplifier** is a high-gain, direct-coupled **differential** amplifier. It amplifies the difference between its two inputs.

<OpAmpTraits />

<Key>Input impedance very high, output impedance very low.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="inverting" title="Figure E7-3: gain is −RF ÷ R1" qs="E7G07 E7G09 E7G10 E7G11">

The **+ input** is grounded, so feedback holds the **− input** at about 0 V. All the input current (Vin ÷ R1) is forced through **RF**, and the output swings the opposite way. **Gain = −RF ÷ R1**. The minus sign means the output is **inverted**.

<Inverting />

For an **absolute** gain, drop the sign and divide: RF ÷ R1. Ask "how many times bigger is RF than R1?"

<Key>Feedback resistor on top, input resistor on the bottom, output is inverted.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="opamp-filter" title="A capacitor across RF turns it into a low-pass filter" qs="E7G02 E7G05">

A capacitor's reactance falls as frequency rises. Across RF, it **shunts the feedback resistor** at high frequencies, so the gain falls: a **low-pass filter**.

<LowPass />

Filter circuits with **high gain and high Q** ring and become unstable. To prevent unwanted ringing and audio instability, **restrict both gain and Q**.

<Key>Capacitor across RF = low-pass. Keep both gain and Q down.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="opamp-real" title="Ideal op-amps don't change with frequency. Real ones do." qs="E7G04 E7G06 E7G08">

An **ideal** op-amp has the same gain at every frequency. A real one loses open-loop gain as frequency rises. The frequency where **open-loop gain falls to one** is its **gain-bandwidth**.

<GainBandwidth />

**Input offset voltage**: ideally both inputs at 0 V give 0 V out. In a real op-amp they don't. The **differential input needed to bring the output to zero** is the offset voltage.

<Offset />

<Key>Gain-bandwidth = frequency where open-loop gain is 1. Offset = input difference that nulls the output.</Key>

</Concept>
