<Concept id="scope-sampling" title="A digital scope only sees its samples" qs="E4A01 E4A06">

A digital oscilloscope's converter grabs snapshots, then connects the dots. The **sampling rate of the analog-to-digital converter** limits the highest frequency it can show accurately. Sample too slowly and you get **aliasing**: a **false, jittery, lower-frequency** waveform.

<Aliasing />

<Key>Fewer than two samples per cycle: the scope invents a slower wave.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="scope-probe" title="Probe habits: compensate, short ground, line trigger" qs="E4A04 E4A09 E4A10">

<ProbeComp />

* **Compensate** on a square wave: trim until the horizontal portions are **as flat as possible**.
* **Keep the probe's ground lead short**. A long one adds inductance and distorts fast signals.
* **Line trigger** locks the sweep to the AC power line, so the **ripple** on a linear supply holds still.

<Key>Flat square wave, short ground, line trigger for ripple.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="spectrum" title="Scope: amplitude over time. Spectrum analyzer: amplitude over frequency" qs="E4A02 E4A03">

<ScopeVsSpectrum />

A **spectrum analyzer** plots **signal amplitude (vertical) against frequency (horizontal)**. Every signal is a spike at its own frequency, so it is the tool for **spurious signals and intermodulation distortion** from an SSB transmitter.

<Key>See other frequencies? Use a spectrum analyzer.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="antenna-analyzer" title="An antenna analyzer reads SWR and impedance directly" qs="E4A07 E4A08 E4A11">

An SWR bridge gives a reading you must interpret. An **antenna analyzer computes SWR and impedance automatically**. It does not tune the antenna for you.

| Instrument | Measures SWR? |
|---|---|
| Directional wattmeter | yes |
| Vector network analyzer | yes |
| Antenna analyzer | yes |

An antenna analyzer can also measure **velocity factor**, **cable length** and the **resonant frequency of a tuned circuit**.

<Key>Measures SWR, all three ways, and more.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="prescaler" title="A prescaler divides a signal down so the counter can count it" qs="E4A05">

<Prescaler />

A prescaler **reduces the signal frequency to within the counter's operating range**. It does not amplify or multiply.

<Key>Prescaler = divider in front of the counter.</Key>

</Concept>
