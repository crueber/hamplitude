<Concept id="radio-horizon" title="The radio horizon is about 15% beyond the geometric horizon" qs="E3C06">

Atmospheric refraction bends VHF/UHF slightly downward, so the **radio horizon is about 15 percent farther** than the geometric horizon. Both grow with the square root of antenna height.

<Horizon />

<Key>Radio horizon: about 15 percent farther.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="flare-cme" title="Flares blackout fast; CMEs storm later" qs="E3C01 E3C07 E3C12">

A **solar flare** sends X-rays that reach us at light speed and cause **short-term radio blackouts**. A **CME** (a cloud of charged particles) arrives later. A sudden rise in HF background noise points to a **CME impact or a solar flare**.

<Chain />

Flare classes run A, B, C, M, X, each **10 times stronger** than the one before.

<FlareClass />

<Key>Flares cause short-term blackouts. X is the greatest class.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="storm-indices" title="Southward Bz and rising K or A mean disturbance" qs="E3C02 E3C03 E3C04 E3C05 E3C08">

**Bz** is the **north-south strength** of the interplanetary magnetic field. **Southward** Bz links up with Earth's field and lets solar-wind energy in, so disturbed conditions become more likely.

<Bz />

A rising **A-index or K-index** means **increasing geomagnetic disturbance**. The extreme storm on the NOAA scale is **G5**. Paths **through the auroral oval** suffer the most absorption.

<Scales />

<Key>Bz south: trouble. K and A rising: more disturbance. G5: extreme.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="tools" title="Reports show what was heard; models and indicators predict" qs="E3C09 E3C10 E3C11">

<Tools />

Reporting networks collect **digital-mode and CW signals** actually heard. **VOACAP** models **HF** propagation. **304 Å** is **UV emission**, tracking the solar flux index.

<Key>Heard: reporting networks. Predicted HF: VOACAP. 304 Å: solar UV.</Key>

</Concept>
