<Concept id="sideband" title="The whole signal must fit, not just the carrier" qs="E1A01 E1A02 E1A03 E1A04">

<E1A_Sideband />

Your dial shows the **carrier**. **USB** extends **above** it, **LSB** extends **below**. Add the bandwidth in the direction the sideband grows and check that end against the edge.

* USB: highest carrier = **upper edge minus bandwidth**
* LSB: lowest carrier = **lower edge plus bandwidth**

<Key>Keep the whole signal inside: move the carrier one signal width (3 kHz for a 3 kHz signal) away from the edge, into the band.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="sixty" title="60 m is channelized: CW goes in the middle" qs="E1A06">

<E1A_Channel />

Channelized 60 m operation puts a CW signal on the **channel's centre frequency**.

</Concept>

<Concept id="lowbands" title="2200 m and 630 m: power is measured as EIRP" qs="E1A07 E1A09">

<E1A_LowBandPower />

Both limits are tiny and are measured as **EIRP** (power as radiated by the antenna, gain included): **1 W** on 2200 m, **5 W** on 630 m (except in some parts of Alaska).

<Mnemonic>2200 m: 1 W. 630 m: 5 W. Both EIRP, not PEP.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="vessel" title="Ships and aircraft: license rules plus the captain's approval" qs="E1A05 E1A10 E1A11">

<E1A_Vessel />

For a US-registered vessel the rules follow the **amateur license**, not a marine permit. The master or pilot only decides whether the station may run at all.

<Key>Any FCC amateur license. Captain approves.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="forwarding" title="Forwarding: the originator answers" qs="E1A08">

<E1A_Forwarding />

Automatic message forwarding passes a message station to station. A bad message that slips through is primarily the fault of the **control operator of the originating station**.

</Concept>
