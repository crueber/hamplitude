<Concept id="reactance" title="FM and PM: vary a capacitance in the oscillator" qs="E7E01 E7E02">

A **reactance modulator** lets the audio change a **capacitance** (often a varactor diode) in the oscillator's tuned circuit. The oscillator's frequency or phase follows the audio, giving **FM or PM**. You apply it to a **local oscillator**, not the final amplifier.

<Reactance />

<Key>Reactance modulator: audio varies a capacitance, so the oscillator makes PM or FM.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="emphasis" title="Boost the highs going in, cut them coming out" qs="E7E05 E7E06">

A **pre-emphasis** network in the transmitter boosts the higher audio frequencies. A **de-emphasis** network in the receiver cuts them back, so the audio is flat again.

<Emphasis />

A phase modulator already produces that rising response, so receivers use de-emphasis for **compatibility with transmitters using phase modulation**.

<Key>Pre-emphasis boosts highs (transmit). De-emphasis cuts them (receive).</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="balanced" title="Balanced modulator cancels the carrier. A filter makes SSB." qs="E7E04 E7E07">

A **balanced modulator** multiplies audio by a carrier and cancels the carrier, leaving two sidebands (DSB). A **sideband filter** then removes one: **single sideband**. The audio before modulation is **baseband**: the frequency range of the message signal.

<BalancedMod />

<Key>Balanced modulator followed by a filter = SSB. Baseband = message range before modulation.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="mixer" title="A mixer outputs both inputs, their sum and their difference" qs="E7E08 E7E09">

Two frequencies in, four principal frequencies out: **each input, the sum, and the difference**. A filter picks the one you want. Overdrive the inputs and the mixer's non-linearity creates extra **spurious products** at other frequencies.

<MixerProducts />

<Key>Mixer out: f1, f2, f1 + f2, f1 − f2. Input too strong: spurious products.</Key>

</Concept>
