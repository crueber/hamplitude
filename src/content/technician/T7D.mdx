<Concept id="meters" title="Voltmeter goes across. Ammeter goes in line." qs="T7D01 T7D02 T7D03 T7D04 T7D07">

<MeterHookup />

| Meter | Measures | Connect |
|---|---|---|
| **Voltmeter** | voltage (electric potential) | **in parallel**, across the part |
| **Ammeter** | current | **in series**, in the path |
| **Ohmmeter** | resistance | across the part, **power off** |

A **multimeter** is all three in one box, set by its dial: it measures **voltage and resistance** (and current).

<Mnemonic>Voltage is between two points, so touch both. Current flows through, so be in the way.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="ohmmeter" title="An ohmmeter makes its own current, so power must be off" qs="T7D05 T7D06 T7D10 T7D11">

<Ohmmeter />

An ohmmeter **applies a small current and measures the resulting voltage**. That's why the circuit must be **unpowered** when you measure resistance in a circuit.

Putting a meter set to **resistance** across a live voltage can **damage it**.

Across a large, discharged capacitor, the meter's current charges it, so the reading **keeps increasing**.

<Key>Ohms: power off. Never measure voltage with the dial on resistance.</Key>

</Concept>
