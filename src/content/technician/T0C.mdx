<Concept id="non-ionizing" title="Radio waves are non-ionizing: they heat, not damage DNA" qs="T0C01 T0C07 T0C12">

<Ionizing />

RF has too little energy to cause chemical changes in cells. Its hazard is **heating**. Touching an antenna while transmitting gives an **RF burn to the skin**.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">"Non-ionizing" does not mean "perfectly safe". RF can still heat tissue, which is why limits exist.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="exposure" title="Exposure depends on frequency, power, distance and pattern" qs="T0C02 T0C04 T0C05 T0C08">

<ExposureFactors />

**All four** matter: frequency, power, distance, and the antenna's radiation pattern. To cut exposure, **relocate the antenna** farther from people.

<MpeCurve />

The limit varies with frequency because the **body absorbs more RF at some frequencies** than others. Of 3.5, 50, 440 and 1296 MHz, **50 MHz** has the lowest limit.

<Key>More power or closer in the beam, more exposure. Limits are tightest around VHF.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="duty-cycle" title="Duty cycle: the time you transmit sets the average" qs="T0C03 T0C10 T0C11">

**Duty cycle** is the **percentage of time a transmitter is transmitting**. Limits use the **average** exposure, so transmitting less lets you run more.

<DutyCycle />

Drop from 100% to 50% and the allowed power density **doubles**.

<Key>Duty cycle = % of time on air. Half the time, double the allowance.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="responsibility" title="The licensee ensures compliance and re-checks after changes" qs="T0C06 T0C09 T0C13">

<Responsibility />

You may check by **calculation** (OET Bulletin 65), **computer modeling** or **measurement** with calibrated equipment. Re-evaluate whenever anything in the transmitter or antenna system changes. SWR and approved-equipment labels aren't the test.

<Key>Responsibility: you, the licensee. Re-evaluate after every change.</Key>

</Concept>
