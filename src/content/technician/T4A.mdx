<Concept id="meters" title="Meters go in the feed line" qs="T4A02 T4A05">

An **RF power meter** reads the power going out to the antenna, so it goes **in the feed line between the transmitter and the antenna**. An accessory SWR meter goes in the same spot.

<Station />

Pick a meter built for the **frequency and power level** you'll measure.

<Key>Meter: in the coax, between radio and antenna.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="power" title="13.8 V, lots of amps, short heavy wire" qs="T4A01 T4A03 T4A09 T4A11">

A mobile radio runs on **13.8 V** DC. A 50 W FM radio draws about **12 A** on transmit. Resistance in thin or long wire wastes voltage as heat: current times resistance.

<PowerFeed />

- **Short, heavy-gauge wire** keeps voltage drop low when you transmit.
- Connect the **negative** lead at the **battery / chassis ground**.

How long a battery lasts:

<Formula label="Runtime in hours">battery amp-hours ÷ average current (A)</Formula>

Example: 100 Ah ÷ 10 A = 10 hours.

<Key>13.8 V at 12 A. Fat wire, short run, negative to battery ground.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="digital" title="Digital modes need three signals" qs="T4A04 T4A06 T4A07">

<DigitalLink />

For FT8 and similar modes the radio's audio goes straight to the **sound card** of a computer running the software. No terminal or converter box needed.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Careful with names">Receive audio comes out of the radio's **speaker connector**, into the computer's **line in**. Push-to-talk is **keying**, not audio.</Callout>

<Key>Receive audio, transmit audio, transmitter keying.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="bonding" title="Bond at RF with wide flat strap" qs="T4A08">

<Strap />

RF current flows on the surface of a conductor, so the more surface, the lower the loss. A **flat copper strap** is preferred for RF bonding.

<Key>Bonding at RF: flat copper strap.</Key>

</Concept>
