<Concept id="overload" title="A strong signal can swamp a receiver that can't reject it" qs="T7B02 T7B03 T7B05 T7B07">

<Overload />

A broadcast radio or TV that picks up your signal has a front end that **can't reject strong signals outside its band**. Your own 2 m radio can suffer the same from a nearby commercial FM station.

Fix it at the **victim receiver's antenna input**: a **band-reject filter** blocks the unwanted signal. Changing your mode doesn't help.

| Cause | What it is |
|---|---|
| Fundamental overload | your signal is simply too strong for their receiver |
| Harmonics | extra signals at multiples of your frequency |
| Spurious emissions | other unwanted signals from a transmitter |

All three can cause RFI (radio frequency interference).

<Key>Block the strong signal at the front door of the receiver that is overloaded.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="first-steps" title="Troubleshoot the simple things first" qs="T7B06 T7B08 T7B09">

| Situation | Do first |
|---|---|
| Neighbor says you interfere with their TV or radio | Check **your own station works properly** and doesn't interfere with your own TV or radio on the same channel |
| Cable TV interference | Check that **all coax connectors are installed properly**. Leaky connectors let your signal in |
| A device in a neighbor's home interferes with you | **All of these**: work with the neighbor to find the device, politely explain FCC rules, and make sure your own station meets good amateur practice |

<Callout kind="warn" title="Never">Don't shut down and call the FCC first, and don't improvise parts like a "harmonic doubler". Start with your own station.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="distortion" title="Distorted audio: too loud, or RF getting into the radio" qs="T7B01 T7B10 T7B11">

On FM, a louder voice (or one closer to the mic) swings the frequency further. Too far is **over-deviation**: it spills past the channel and sounds distorted.

<Deviation />

Fix: **talk farther from the microphone**. Being slightly off frequency or in a bad location can also garble your audio.

The other cause is **RF feedback**: your own signal gets onto the mic cable and back into the transmitter.

<RfFeedback />

<Key>Too much voice? Back off the mic. RF in the cable? Clip on a ferrite choke.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="swr-power" title="High SWR can cut your power output" qs="T7B04">

A poor antenna match reflects power back toward a solid-state transmitter. To protect itself, it **turns its output power down**.

<SwrWaves />

<Key>Low output power from a solid-state radio: suspect high SWR.</Key>

</Concept>
