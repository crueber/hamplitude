<Concept id="multipath" title="Several paths add up, or cancel" qs="T3A01 T3A05 T3A06 T3A08 T3A10">

A signal often arrives by a **direct path** *and* by reflections off buildings, hills or the ionosphere. The paths differ in length, so the waves arrive out of step: they **add** or **cancel**.

<Multipath />

- Move the antenna a few feet and the sum swings strong to weak.
- **Picket fencing**: rapid flutter on a moving (mobile) signal.
- **Fading** on ionospheric signals: random combining of paths.
- **Data**: delayed copies corrupt bits, so error rates rise.
- Blocked line of sight? Aim at a **reflecting path** instead.

<Key>Multipath: reinforce or cancel, depending on a few feet of position.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="polarization" title="Match polarization on line-of-sight paths" qs="T3A03 T3A04 T3A09">

Polarization is the direction of the electric field. On a straight path, a transmitting and receiving antenna must be **parallel**. **Cross-polarized** (one vertical, one horizontal) **reduces signal strength**.

<Polarization />

| Path | Polarization |
|---|---|
| Long-distance **CW and SSB** on VHF/UHF | **Horizontal** (FM and repeaters use vertical) |
| Via the **ionosphere** | Wave becomes elliptical, so **either** works |

<Key>Same orientation at both ends. Weak-signal VHF/UHF: horizontal.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="absorption" title="Short waves are soaked up by water and leaves" qs="T3A02 T3A07 T3A12">

<Absorb />

| Conditions | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Vegetation**, UHF and microwave | Absorbs signals; weak ones suffer |
| **Precipitation**, microwave | Decreases range |
| **Fog or rain**, 10 m and 6 m | **Little effect** |

<Key>The higher the frequency, the more water and foliage matter.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="ionosphere" title="The ionosphere reflects HF" qs="T3A11">

The **ionosphere** is a layer of charged air high above the Earth. It bends HF waves back down, so they come down far away. The troposphere (weather layer) and stratosphere do not do this.

<IonosphereBounce />

<Key>HF reflects off the ionosphere.</Key>

</Concept>
