<Concept id="purpose" title="The FCC makes the rules; the goal is radio skill" qs="T1A01 T1A02">

The **FCC** writes and enforces Part 97, the amateur rules. The service exists to **advance technical and communication skills** in the radio art.

| Who | Role |
|---|---|
| **FCC** | Regulates and enforces |
| **ARRL** | National hobby group, not a regulator |
| **ITU** | International spectrum coordination |

<Key>Rules come from the FCC. Purpose: advance radio skills.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="grant" title="The FCC database is your proof of license" qs="T1A04 T1A05">

<T1A_LicenseGrant />

The FCC **emails** you a link to download the grant. The proof you're licensed is your entry in the FCC **ULS** (Universal Licensing System) database.

<Key>Email from the FCC, proof in the database.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="special" title="Space stations and beacons have exact definitions" qs="T1A06 T1A07">

<T1A_SpaceStation />

A **beacon** is an automatically controlled station whose signal lets others judge propagation (is the band open?). On HF, Technicians find them on 10 m.

<T1B_TenMeter focus="beacon" />

<Key>Space station: over 50 km up. HF beacons: 28.200 to 28.300 MHz.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="coordinator" title="Coordinators and RACES: who authorizes what" qs="T1A08 T1A09 T1A10">

<T1A_Coordinator />

A **frequency coordinator** is a volunteer chosen by local amateurs. Coordinators recommend repeater and auxiliary channels so stations don't collide.

<T1A_Races />

**RACES** (Radio Amateur Civil Emergency Service) is run through civil defense, so its control operators need that organization's enrollment.

</Concept>
