<Concept id="resistor" title="A resistor opposes current. A potentiometer adjusts it." qs="T6A01 T6A02 T6A03">

A **resistor** opposes the flow of current. A **potentiometer** is a resistor with a moving wiper: turn the knob and the **resistance** changes. That makes it the usual **volume control**.

<Potentiometer />

<Key>Potentiometer = adjustable resistor. What it controls is resistance.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="storage" title="Capacitor: electric field. Inductor: magnetic field." qs="T6A04 T6A05 T6A06 T6A07">

Both store energy, in different fields. Their build tells you which is which.

<StoreEnergy />

| Part | Built from | Stores energy in |
|---|---|---|
| **Capacitor** | conductive plates with an insulator between | an **electric** field |
| **Inductor** | a coil of wire | a **magnetic** field |

<Mnemonic>Capacitor = plates = electric field. Inductor = coil = magnetic field.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="switches" title="Poles are circuits. Throws are positions." qs="T6A08 T6A09">

**Pole** = how many separate circuits the switch controls. **Throw** = how many positions each circuit can be switched to.

<Switches />

Exam figure T-2 shows a switch with one blade and a single gap: **SPST**, a plain on/off switch.

<Key>SPDT: one circuit switched between one of two others.</Key>

</Concept>
