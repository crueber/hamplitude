<Concept id="fields" title="A radio wave is two fields, at right angles" qs="T3B01 T3B02 T3B03">

A radio wave has two parts: an **electric field** and a **magnetic field**. They move together, **90° apart**.

<EmWave caption="Red = electric field. Blue = magnetic field. Both are perpendicular to the direction of travel." />

**Polarization** is just the direction of the *electric* field. Electric field up‑down → vertical polarization.

<Key>Two fields, at right angles. Polarization = direction of the electric field.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="speed" title="Every radio wave travels at the speed of light" qs="T3B04 T3B11 T3B12">

In free space, **all** radio waves move at the speed of light: about **300,000,000 meters per second**.

<SameSpeed />

HF, VHF, microwaves, even visible light: same speed. Only the frequency and wavelength differ.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Watch the units">Light is about 300,000,000 **meters** per second. The miles-per-hour choices are too slow, and sound is roughly a million times slower than radio.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="wavelength" title="Higher frequency, shorter wavelength" qs="T3B05 T3B06 T3B07">

**Wavelength (λ)** is the distance one full wave takes up. Since the speed is fixed, more wiggles per second means each wiggle is shorter.

<Formula label="Wavelength in meters">λ = 300 ÷ f (MHz)</Formula>

<WavelengthExplorer />

Try 146 MHz: 300 ÷ 146 ≈ **2 meters**. That's why it's the "2‑meter band". Bands are named by their wavelength.

<Key>Frequency up → wavelength down. Divide 300 by the frequency in MHz.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="ranges" title="HF, VHF and UHF: each is ×10 the last" qs="T3B08 T3B09 T3B10">

<SpectrumBar highlight={['HF', 'VHF', 'UHF']} />

| Name | Range | Think |
|---|---|---|
| **HF** | 3 – 30 MHz | **H**igh |
| **VHF** | 30 – 300 MHz | **V**ery high |
| **UHF** | 300 – 3000 MHz | **U**ltra high |

<Mnemonic>3‑30, 30‑300, 300‑3000. Each step multiplies by 10, and the first digit stays 3.</Mnemonic>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">Wrong answers use the right numbers with **kHz** instead of MHz. Check the unit.</Callout>

</Concept>
