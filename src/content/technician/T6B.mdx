<Concept id="diode" title="A diode is a one-way valve for current" qs="T6B02 T6B06 T6B09">

A **diode** lets current flow in **one direction only**. Its two electrodes are the **anode** (current goes in) and the **cathode** (current goes out). On the real part, a **stripe** marks the cathode.

<DiodeValve />

<Mnemonic>The symbol's triangle is an arrow: current goes with the arrow, from anode to the bar (cathode).</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="led" title="An LED lights with forward current, and every diode drops some voltage" qs="T6B01 T6B07">

An **LED** is a diode that glows. Current has to flow **forward** through it: reverse it and nothing lights.

<DiodeValve led />

A conducting diode always uses up a little voltage, its **forward voltage drop**. It is normal, it varies by diode type, and it is not a fault.

| Type | Typical forward drop |
|---|---|
| Schottky diode | about 0.3 V |
| Silicon diode | about 0.7 V |
| LED | about 2 V or more, by colour |

<Key>LED light comes from forward current. Forward drop is normal and differs between diode types.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="transistor-parts" title="Transistors: three regions, three leads" qs="T6B04 T6B05 T6B08 T6B12">

A transistor has **three leads**, and the common bipolar kind is built from **three regions** of semiconductor. One lead is the control; the other two carry the main current.

<TransistorParts />

| Family | Leads |
|---|---|
| Bipolar junction (BJT) | **emitter, base, collector** |
| Field-effect (FET) | **source, gate, drain** |

<Mnemonic>Gate, drain, source: a FET has a **gate**. A BJT has a **base**.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="gain" title="A small signal controls a bigger one: that is gain" qs="T6B03 T6B10 T6B11">

A transistor uses a **small** base (or gate) signal to control a **much larger** current. Use it fully on or off and it is an **electronic switch**. Keep it in between and it **amplifies**.

<TransistorGain />

**Gain** is output ÷ input. It can be measured in **voltage, current or power**, so all three are gain.

<Key>Only active parts such as transistors give power gain. Transformers, resistors and reactors cannot.</Key>

</Concept>
