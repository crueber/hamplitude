<Concept id="shock" title="Current through you hurts; stored charge still bites" qs="T0A02 T0A06 T0A11 T0A12">

Current through the body **heats tissue**, **disrupts cells** and **clenches muscles**. Every one of those is a hazard.

<PowerSupplyHazards />

Do **all** of these: use three-wire cords, bond equipment to a common safety ground, and **discharge capacitors** before working inside. A power supply's filter capacitors keep their charge after switch-off. When measuring high voltage, use a meter and leads **rated for that voltage**.

<Key>Off is not safe: capacitors hold charge. Ground everything. Rate your meter.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="fuse" title="Fuses go in the hot wire, sized to the wiring" qs="T0A03 T0A04 T0A05 T0A08">

In US 120 V cable **black is hot**, white is neutral, green is the equipment ground. A fuse or breaker goes **in series with the hot wire only**, and removes power when current is too high.

<AcWiring />

Use the **rated** fuse. A 20 A fuse won't blow at currents that already overheat 5 A wiring, so the wire overheats: **fire**.

<FuseFire />

<Key>Fuse = in series, hot wire, correct rating. Oversized fuse = fire.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="battery" title="Batteries: never short them, never rush them" qs="T0A01 T0A10">

A 12 V battery without protection circuitry can deliver huge current. **Shorting** the terminals gives burns, fire or an explosion. **Rapid charge or discharge** overheats it and makes it **out-gas**.

<BatteryHazards />

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">The danger is the short circuit, not shock from touching both terminals.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="lightning" title="Arrester at the entry, and bond every ground" qs="T0A07 T0A09">

<LightningEntry />

Put the **lightning arrester** on a **grounded panel where feed lines enter** the building. Then **bond all external ground rods together** with heavy wire or strap.

<Key>Arrester at the entry panel. All grounds bonded.</Key>

</Concept>
