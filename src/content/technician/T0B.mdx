<Concept id="climbing" title="Climb trained, tied off, and never alone" qs="T0B02 T0B03 T0B07">

<ClimbRules />

**All** apply when climbing: training, an approved harness with tie-off at all times, and a helper or observer. There's no height or job type that makes it safe alone: the answer to "when can I climb alone?" is **never**.

A **crank-up tower** must not be climbed unless it is **retracted** or **mechanical safety locks** are installed.

<Key>Training, harness, helper. Alone? Never.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="power-lines" title="If it falls, it must stay 10 feet from power wires" qs="T0B04 T0B06 T0B09">

Before raising anything, look for **overhead wires** and stay clear. Plan for the worst: if the antenna or tower falls toward the line, **no part may come within 10 feet** of the wires.

<PowerLineClear />

For the same reason, **never attach an antenna to a utility pole**: it could contact high-voltage lines.

<Key>Fall zone plus 10 feet. Utility poles are off limits.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="tower-ground" title="Ground each leg, bonded, short and direct" qs="T0B01 T0B08 T0B10 T0B11">

<TowerGround />

Drive a **separate 8-foot rod for each tower leg**, bonded to the tower and to each other. Keep ground wires **short and direct**, and avoid sharp bends. **Local electrical codes** set the grounding requirements, not FCC Part 97.

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">A drip loop, right-angle bends, a single short rod or a water pipe can look reasonable. None is the proper method.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="turnbuckle" title="A safety wire keeps a turnbuckle from backing off" qs="T0B05">

<Turnbuckle />

Wind vibration slowly turns turnbuckles. A **safety wire** through it **prevents loosening from vibration**.

<Key>Safety wire = vibration lock, not a lightning path.</Key>

</Concept>
