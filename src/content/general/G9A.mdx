<Concept id="z0" title="Characteristic impedance comes from the line's shape" qs="G9A01 G9A03">

A line's **characteristic impedance (Z₀)** is set by its **geometry**: the **spacing** between conductors and their **radius**. Wider spacing raises it, fatter conductors lower it. Length and frequency don't change it.

<G9A_Z0Geometry />

| Line | Nominal Z₀ |
|---|---|
| Coax | 50 Ω (or 75 Ω) |
| **Window line** (ladder line with windows in its web) | **450 Ω** |

<Key>Spacing and radius set Z₀. Window line is 450 Ω.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="swr-calc" title="SWR is the bigger impedance divided by the smaller" qs="G9A04 G9A07 G9A09 G9A10">

Power reflects wherever the **load impedance differs from the line's Z₀**. For a resistive load, **SWR = larger ÷ smaller**, so it is never below 1:1. Only a **perfect match** gives no standing waves.

<G9A_SwrLoad />

| Load on 50 Ω line | Working | SWR |
|---|---|---|
| 200 Ω | 200 ÷ 50 | **4:1** |
| 10 Ω | 50 ÷ 10 | **5:1** |
| 50 Ω | equal | 1:1 |

<Key>Larger ÷ smaller. Match the load to Z₀ for no standing waves.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="loss" title="Feed line loss rises with frequency, in dB per 100 feet" qs="G9A05 G9A06">

Cable loss is quoted in **decibels per 100 feet**. Coax loses **more as frequency rises**, so a long run that is fine on 80 m can be costly on 70 cm.

<CableLoss />

<G9A_LossDb />

<Key>dB per 100 ft. Higher frequency, more attenuation.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="line-swr" title="High SWR adds loss, but loss and tuners hide it" qs="G9A02 G9A08 G9A11">

Reflected power travels the line again, so in a lossy line **a high SWR increases loss**. Loss also **absorbs the reflection** on its way back, so the SWR at the **radio end reads lower** than at the antenna.

A **matching network at the transmitter** can make the radio see 1:1, but the mismatch is still at the antenna, so the line still carries the **same SWR**.

<G9A_LineSwr />

<Key>Tuners and loss change what the radio sees, not the antenna mismatch.</Key>

</Concept>
