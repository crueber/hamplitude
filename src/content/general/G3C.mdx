<Concept id="layers" title="From the ground up: D, E, F1, F2. Higher means farther" qs="G3C01 G3C03">

<Layers />

The **D** region is closest to Earth. **F2** is the highest, so a hop off it lands the farthest. Height, not density, is the reason.

<Key>D lowest, F2 highest, F2 skips farthest.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="d-region" title="By day the D region swallows the low bands" qs="G3C05 G3C11">

<DayNight />

The **D region** is a daytime layer that **absorbs signals below 10 MHz**, which includes the 160, 80, 60 and 40 m bands. Long-distance contacts there are harder by day. At night D fades and the F region returns them.

<Key>Daytime, D absorbs. Not the E or F region.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="critical" title="Too high a frequency or too steep an angle goes straight through" qs="G3C02 G3C04">

Each layer can refract only so hard. The **critical frequency** at a given incidence angle is the **highest frequency refracted back to Earth**. The **critical angle** is the **highest takeoff angle** that still returns the wave at a given frequency.

<CriticalAngle />

<Key>Highest frequency, highest angle that still return. Beyond either: gone.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="scatter" title="Scatter fills the skip zone weakly, with flutter" qs="G3C06 G3C07 G3C08 G3C09">

Skywave skips over a **skip zone**. **Scatter** is the way you can still hear a signal there: a small part of the energy is scattered back down.

<Scatter />

Only a **small part** of the energy goes that way, so scatter signals are **weak**. Energy arrives over **several different paths**, so the audio is **distorted** with a **fluttering** sound.

<Key>Weak and fluttery: scatter into the skip zone.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="nvis" title="NVIS: aim steeply up and cover the area around you" qs="G3C10">

**Near vertical incidence skywave** is **short-distance MF or HF propagation at high elevation angles**. The signal goes almost straight up and comes back nearby, so there is **no skip zone**.

<Nvis />

<Key>NVIS = high angle, short distance, no gap.</Key>

</Concept>
