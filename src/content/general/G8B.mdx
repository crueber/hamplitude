<Concept id="mixer" title="A mixer outputs the sum and the difference. Tune the LO to pick the signal." qs="G8B11 G8B03 G8B01 G8B02">

Mixing two RF signals is **heterodyning**. Out come the **sum and difference** of the two inputs, not their average, product or ratio. A receiver keeps the difference as a fixed **IF** (intermediate frequency). To receive a different signal, **tune the local oscillator (LO)**, so a different input lands on the IF.

<G8B_Mixer />

A second signal on the other side of the LO is also one IF away, so it lands on the IF too. This **image response** sits **twice the IF** from the desired signal.

<Key>Mixer output = sum and difference. LO tunes. Image = 2 × IF from the wanted signal.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="multiplier" title="A multiplier scales the frequency and the deviation together" qs="G8B04 G8B07">

A VHF FM transmitter often starts at a low frequency and uses a **multiplier** stage, which generates a **harmonic** of it, to reach the operating frequency. The frequency swing is multiplied too, so the oscillator swings by the final deviation divided by the multiplication factor.

<G8B_Multiplier />

<Formula label="Oscillator deviation">factor = 146.52 ÷ 12.21 = 12, so deviation = 5 kHz ÷ 12 = 416.7 Hz</Formula>

<Key>Final deviation ÷ multiplication factor = oscillator deviation.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="fm-bandwidth" title="FM bandwidth: double the sum of deviation and voice frequency" qs="G8B06">

As a rule of thumb (Carson's rule), the signal reaches the deviation **plus** the modulating frequency on each side of the carrier. Count one side, then double it.

<G8B_FmBandwidth />

<Formula label="FM phone bandwidth">2 × (deviation + modulating frequency) = 2 × (5 + 3) = **16 kHz**</Formula>

<Key>Double the sum. Do not stop at 5 + 3 = 8.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="bandwidth-rules" title="Faster symbols need more bandwidth. Match the receiver to the signal." qs="G8B10 G8B09">

Faster symbol rate means a **wider** signal. A receiver filter matched to the mode passes the whole signal and little else, so it gives the **best signal-to-noise ratio**. It is not an FCC rule, and it has nothing to do with impedance or power draw.

<Key>Higher symbol rate = wider. Matched filter = best signal-to-noise.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="duty-cycle" title="Duty cycle is how much of the time you transmit" qs="G8B08">

A mode that transmits nearly continuously has a **high duty cycle**, so the **average power** is close to the key-down power. That can exceed the transmitter's **average power rating** even when the peak is fine.

<G8B_DutyCycle />

<Key>Know the duty cycle so a high-duty mode doesn't overheat your transmitter.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="intermod" title="Intermodulation: unwanted mixing, and odd orders land closest" qs="G8B12 G8B05 G8B13">

A mixer mixes **on purpose**. **Intermodulation** is the same mixing in a **non-linear circuit** that produces unwanted spurious outputs. The **order** is the sum of the multipliers: 2F1−F2 is 2 + 1 = 3, an **odd** order.

<G8B_Intermod />

| Product | Order | Odd or even |
|---|---|---|
| 2F1−F2 | 3 | **odd** |
| 3F1−F2 | 4 | even |
| 5F1−3F2 | 8 | even |

<Key>Odd-order products (3rd, 5th) are the ones closest to your signals.</Key>

</Concept>
