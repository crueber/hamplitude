<Concept id="rfi-fixes" title="RF on audio cables: block it or shunt it" qs="G4C01 G4C08">

Cables pick up your RF as **common-mode current**: the same RF on every conductor. Two fixes:

- **Ferrite choke** on the cable blocks common-mode current.
- **Bypass capacitor** passes RF to ground but leaves the audio alone.

<RfiPaths />

Shorting the center conductor to the shield or grounding it would also short the audio. Extra insulation does nothing.

<Key>Choke the cable, or bypass the RF to ground.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="rfi-sound" title="The sound tells you what is interfering" qs="G4C02 G4C03 G4C04">

<Heard />

**SSB** has no steady carrier, so the audio device detects a **distorted** version of the speech. **CW** switches the carrier on and off, so you hear on-and-off humming or clicking. A wide range of frequencies points to **arcing at a poor electrical connection**.

<Key>Garbled speech: SSB. Keyed thumps: CW. Wideband noise: an arc.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="rf-ground" title="A ground wire is not a ground at RF" qs="G4C05 G4C06">

At RF a wire has **impedance**, not just resistance. If the ground wire has **high impedance at your frequency**, RF voltage appears on equipment and can cause **RF burns**. A **resonant** ground wire is the worst case: it presents high impedance at the equipment and puts **high RF voltage on enclosures**.

<GroundZ />

<Key>Keep RF ground wires short, and never resonant on a band you use.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="bonding" title="Bond enclosures together, and ground them all" qs="G4C07 G4C09 G4C10 G4C11 G4C12">

<Bonding />

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Ground loops (hum on **transmitted audio**) | **Bond equipment enclosures together** |
| RF "hot spots" | **Bond all enclosures together** |
| Why ground every metal enclosure | hazardous voltage **cannot appear on the chassis** |
| Lightning-protection grounds | **No solder**: a strike's heat can destroy the joint |

<Key>Bond everything together; ground it for safety; never solder a lightning ground.</Key>

</Concept>
