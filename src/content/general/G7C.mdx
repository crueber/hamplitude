<Concept id="ssb-chain" title="SSB: balanced modulator, filter, and a product detector to undo it" qs="G7C01 G7C02 G7C04">

A **balanced modulator** mixes audio with a carrier and cancels the carrier. Its output is **double-sideband** RF. A **filter** then selects one sideband. In an SSB receiver a **product detector** mixes the signal with a carrier to get the audio back.

<SsbChain />

<Key>Balanced modulator: double-sideband RF. Filter: picks the sideband. Product detector: SSB receiver, extracts the audio.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="match-xfmr" title="A matching transformer shows each side the impedance it wants" qs="G7C03">

A transmitter output stage works best into one impedance, the feed line wants another. A **transformer** presents the **desired impedance to the transmitter and the feed line**. Impedance changes with the **square** of the turns ratio.

<MatchXfmr />

<Key>Impedance-matching transformer: the right impedance for both the transmitter and the feed line.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="sensitivity" title="Sensitivity: gain, noise figure and bandwidth all matter" qs="G7C08">

A receiver hears a signal only if it rises above its own noise. That noise floor depends on the **bandwidth** (wider lets in more noise) and the **noise figure** (how much noise the input stage adds). **Input gain** boosts the signal ahead of later, noisier stages. All three affect sensitivity.

<Sensitivity />

<Key>Input amplifier gain, input amplifier noise figure and demodulator bandwidth all affect sensitivity.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="filter-terms" title="Filter vocabulary: read it off the response curve" qs="G7C07 G7C12 G7C13 G7C14">

Pick a filter type and read each term off the curve. **Half-power** is the −3 dB point.

<FilterResponse />

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Insertion loss** | attenuation **inside the passband** |
| **Cutoff frequency** | where a low-pass output power falls to half the input |
| **Bandwidth** (band-pass) | between the **upper and lower half-power** points |
| **Ultimate rejection** | the **maximum** attenuation outside the passband |

<Mnemonic>Insertion = inside. Ultimate = the most it can reject.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="dds" title="A DDS tunes digitally but is as stable as its crystal" qs="G7C05">

A **direct digital synthesizer** counts with a fixed **crystal clock**. A **tuning word** sets how fast the count advances, and a table and DAC turn it into a sine wave. Change the word and the output frequency changes.

<DdsBlocks />

<Key>DDS = variable output frequency with the stability of a crystal oscillator.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="sdr-dsp" title="SDR: two signals 90° apart, then software does the rest" qs="G7C06 G7C09 G7C10 G7C11">

An SDR mixes the signal with an oscillator at **0°** and at **90°**. These two are the **I** and **Q** signals. An **ADC** digitizes them and software does the **filtering, detection and modulation**.

<SdrChain />

I and Q together set amplitude and phase, so with the right processing **all types of modulation** can be created.

<IqPhasor />

A **DSP filter** is just numbers, so one radio offers a **wide range of bandwidths and shapes** without changing any parts.

| | Analog filter | DSP filter |
|---|---|---|
| Bandwidth and shape | fixed by its parts | set by software |

<Key>I and Q: 90° apart. Any modulation. Filtering, detection and modulation all in software.</Key>

</Concept>
