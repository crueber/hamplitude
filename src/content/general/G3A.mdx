<Concept id="sunspots" title="More sunspots, more ionization, higher usable frequencies" qs="G3A01 G3A04 G3A07 G3A10">

Sunspots mark an active Sun that emits more ultraviolet and X-rays. These ionize the **F region** more strongly, so it can bend **higher frequencies** back to Earth. Drag the slider.

<SunChain />

At the cycle's low, **15, 12 and 10 m** are the least reliable long-distance bands. **20 m** supports daylight worldwide paths at any point. Zero sunspots does not kill propagation, it mainly weakens the highest bands. The Sun rotates in **26 to 28 days**, so conditions repeat on that rhythm.

<Key>High sunspots, high bands. 20 m works at any point in the cycle.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="arrival" title="Flares arrive in 8 minutes, particle clouds in hours to days" qs="G3A02 G3A03 G3A11 G3A14">

A flare's **UV and X-rays travel at light speed**: about **8 minutes**. They ionize the **sunlit side**, adding absorption that hits **lower frequencies hardest** (a sudden ionospheric disturbance). **Charged particles** are slow. A coronal mass ejection takes **15 hours to several days**. Coronal hole particles **disturb HF**.

<Arrival />

<Key>Light: 8 minutes. Particles: hours to days.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="indices" title="SFI watches the Sun; K and A watch Earth's magnetic field" qs="G3A05 G3A12 G3A13">

<Indices />

**SFI** is the Sun's radio noise at **10.7 cm**, a measure of solar radiation. The **K index** is the **short-term** stability of Earth's geomagnetic field. The **A index** is the **long-term** one.

<Mnemonic>K = short, A = long. Neither is a sunspot count.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="storms" title="A geomagnetic storm hurts high-latitude HF but lights up VHF aurora" qs="G3A06 G3A08 G3A09">

A **geomagnetic storm** is a temporary disturbance of Earth's field. It **degrades high-latitude HF** paths. The same activity makes **aurora**, which can **reflect VHF** signals.

<Storm />

<Key>Storm: polar HF down, aurora VHF up.</Key>

</Concept>
