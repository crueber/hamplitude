<Concept id="yagi" title="A Yagi: long reflector, half-wave driven, short directors" qs="G9C02 G9C03 G9C07 G9C08">

The **driven element** is about **½ wavelength**. A **reflector** slightly **longer** sits behind it, and **directors** slightly **shorter** sit in front. The **main lobe** is the direction of maximum radiated field. **Front-to-back ratio** compares the main lobe to the signal radiated the opposite way.

<G9C_YagiRoles />

<Key>Reflector longer, directors shorter. Main lobe forward.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="tuning" title="More directors, more gain; element size sets bandwidth" qs="G9C05 G9C10 G9C01">

Adding **directors** and lengthening the **boom** raises **gain** and narrows the beam. Gain, front-to-back ratio and SWR bandwidth all depend on **boom length, number of elements and element spacing**, so designers trade between them. **Larger-diameter elements** widen the SWR bandwidth.

<G9C_Bandwidth />

<Key>Longer boom, more directors: more gain. Fatter elements: more bandwidth.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="gain-units" title="dBi is 2.15 dB bigger than dBd; stacking adds 3 dB" qs="G9C04 G9C09">

**dBi** measures gain against an isotropic radiator, **dBd** against a half-wave dipole. A dipole is already **2.15 dBi**, so the same antenna reads **2.15 higher in dBi**.

<G9C_DbiDbd />

Stack two identical Yagis **½ λ apart** and you get about **twice the power**: 10 log 2 ≈ **3 dB**.

<G9C_Stack />

<Key>dBi = dBd + 2.15. Double the antennas, plus 3 dB.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="match" title="Hairpin and gamma matches fit a Yagi to the feed line" qs="G9C11 G9C12">

A Yagi's feed point is lower than a feed line's impedance, so it needs a match. A **hairpin (beta) match** is a **shorted stub** across the split driven element. A **gamma match** uses a **rod and a series capacitor**, and the driven element can be **bonded to the boom**.

<G9C_Matches />

<Key>Hairpin: shorted stub. Gamma: no insulation from the boom.</Key>

</Concept>
