<Concept id="reactance" title="Reactance: how coils and capacitors oppose AC" qs="G5A02 G5A03 G5A04 G5A05 G5A06 G5A09 G5A11">

**Reactance** (**X**) is opposition to **alternating** current caused by an inductor or a capacitor. It is measured in **ohms**. It depends on **frequency**, not on how big the signal is.

<G5A_ReactanceCurves />

- **Inductor**: higher frequency, **more** reactance (XL).
- **Capacitor**: higher frequency, **less** reactance (XC).

<Key>Coil: reactance rises with frequency. Capacitor: reactance falls. Both in ohms, symbol X.</Key>

<Mnemonic>An inductor resists *change*, and faster AC changes more. A capacitor passes fast AC more easily.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="impedance" title="Impedance is voltage over current" qs="G5A07 G5A08">

**Impedance** (**Z**) is the total opposition to AC: resistance and reactance together. It is the AC version of Ohm's law, voltage divided by current.

<G5A_ImpedanceTerms />

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Impedance** Z | E ÷ I, in ohms |
| **Admittance** Y | the inverse of impedance |
| Conductance, susceptance | inverses of resistance, reactance |
| Reluctance | opposition in a *magnetic* path |

<Key>Impedance = voltage ÷ current. Its inverse is admittance.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="resonance" title="At resonance, XL and XC cancel" qs="G5A01 G5A12">

XL rises with frequency and XC falls, so at one frequency they are **equal**. In a **series** LC circuit they cancel, leaving impedance **very low**: current flows easily.

<G5A_Resonance />

<Key>Resonance: XL = XC, they cancel. Series LC impedance is very low.</Key>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">It is the *reactances* that cancel, not resistance, and not voltage and current.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="matching" title="Matching changes one impedance into another" qs="G5A10">

A transformer, a Pi-network and a length of transmission line can all make a load look like a different impedance to the source.

<G5A_Matching />

<Key>Transformer, Pi-network, transmission line: all can match impedances.</Key>

</Concept>
