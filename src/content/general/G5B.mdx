<Concept id="decibels" title="3 dB doubles power. 1 dB loses about a fifth." qs="G5B01 G5B10">

Decibels compare power as a ratio. **+3 dB** is two times the power, **−3 dB** is half. Losses compound: a loss of **1 dB** leaves about 79 % of the power, so about **20.6 %** is gone.

<DecibelExplorer />

<G5B_DbLoss />

<Key>3 dB = ×2 power. 1 dB loss = 20.6 % of the power lost.</Key>

<Callout kind="warn" title="Trap">6 dB is two doublings, so ×4 in power. 3 dB is the single doubling.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="parallel-current" title="Parallel branches: same voltage, currents add" qs="G5B02">

Every branch sees the full source voltage and draws its own current, **I = E ÷ R**. The source supplies the **sum**. The smaller resistor takes the bigger share, which is how a **current divider** splits current. Across series parts, voltage divides instead, with the larger resistor getting the bigger share.

<G5B_ParallelCurrents />

<Key>Total current = sum of the branch currents. More branches means more total current.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="power" title="Power: pick the formula for what you know" qs="G5B03 G5B04 G5B05 G5B12">

Power is **P = E × I**. Ohm's law (E = I × R) gives two more forms when one of E or I is missing.

<G5B_PowerCalc />

| You know | Formula | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 12 V, 0.2 A | P = E × I | **2.4 W** |
| 400 V, 800 Ω | P = E² ÷ R | **200 W** |
| 7 mA, 1,250 Ω | P = I² × R, with I = 0.007 A | **61 mW** |
| 1,200 W, 50 Ω | E = √(P × R) | **245 V** |

<Mnemonic>Convert milliamperes to amperes first, then square.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="rms" title="RMS is the heating value: 0.707 of peak" qs="G5B07 G5B08 G5B09">

An AC signal swings between its **peak** values. Its **RMS** value heats a resistor exactly like the same DC voltage. For a sine wave:

<G5B_SineLevels />

<Key>RMS = 0.707 × peak. Peak = 1.414 × RMS. Peak-to-peak = 2 × peak.</Key>

<Callout kind="tip" title="Check direction">RMS is always the smallest of the three. If your answer is smaller than the RMS you started with, you went the wrong way.</Callout>

</Concept>

<Concept id="pep" title="PEP: power at the crest of the signal" qs="G5B06 G5B11 G5B13 G5B14">

**Peak envelope power (PEP)** is the power at the highest point of the signal's envelope. A scope reads peak-to-peak, so convert to RMS, then use E² ÷ R.

<G5B_PepSteps />

An unmodulated carrier never changes amplitude, so its **PEP equals its average power**: ratio **1.00**.

<Key>Peak-to-peak ÷ 2 × 0.707 = RMS. Then P = E² ÷ R. Steady carrier: PEP = average.</Key>

</Concept>
