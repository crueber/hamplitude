<Concept id="battery" title="Low internal resistance means more current and less sag" qs="G6A01 G6A02">

A battery is an ideal source in series with a small **internal resistance**. Load current drops voltage across it, so the terminal voltage sags and heat is wasted. A **low** internal resistance sags less, so the battery can deliver a **high discharge current**.

<BatteryResistance />

<Key>Low internal resistance = high discharge current. For maximum life, never run a 12 V lead-acid battery below **10.5 V**.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="diode-threshold" title="A diode conducts only above its threshold voltage" qs="G6A03 G6A05">

Forward voltage below the **threshold** passes almost no current. Just above it, current climbs steeply. The threshold depends on the material.

<DiodeKnee />

| Diode | Forward threshold |
|---|---|
| Germanium | **0.3 V** |
| Silicon | **0.7 V** |

<Mnemonic>Silicon is the common one: 0.7. Germanium is lower, about half: 0.3.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>

<Concept id="real-parts" title="Real parts have hidden extras, and capacitor types differ" qs="G6A04 G6A06 G6A08 G6A11">

A wire-wound resistor is a coil, so it has **inductance**: unpredictable at RF. Every coil also has a little **turn-to-turn capacitance**. Above its **self-resonant frequency** an inductor stops acting like an inductor and becomes **capacitive**.

<RealParts />

Capacitor types are chosen for what they do best:

| Type | Known for |
|---|---|
| **Electrolytic** | **high capacitance for a given volume** (not tight tolerance or low leakage) |
| **Low-voltage ceramic** | **low cost** (not tight tolerance or high stability) |

<Key>Wire-wound resistor: inductance, so avoid at RF. Inductor above self-resonance: capacitive.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="transistor-regions" title="A transistor switch uses cutoff and saturation, a MOSFET gate is insulated" qs="G6A07 G6A09">

A bipolar transistor has three operating regions. Fully off is **cutoff**, fully on is **saturation**, and in between is the **active** region where it amplifies. A **switch** uses only the two ends. Drag the base current to see.

<TransistorSwitch />

A **MOSFET** controls its channel with an electric field. Its gate is separated from the channel by a **thin insulating layer**.

<Mosfet />

<Key>Switch = saturation and cutoff. MOSFET gate = insulated by a thin layer.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="vacuum-tube" title="Vacuum tube: grids steer the electron flow" qs="G6A10 G6A12">

A heated **cathode** boils off electrons toward the positive **plate**. The **control grid** between them regulates how many get through. The **screen grid** sits between control grid and plate and **reduces grid-to-plate capacitance**.

<TubeAnatomy />

<Mnemonic>Control grid = the control. Screen grid = the screen (shield) between input and output.</Mnemonic>

</Concept>
