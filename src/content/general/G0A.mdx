<Concept id="averaging" title="RF heats tissue; exposure is power density averaged over time" qs="G0A01 G0A02 G0A04 G0A07">

RF energy **heats body tissue**. Exposure depends on **power density, frequency and duty cycle**: all three. **Time averaging** is the total exposure averaged over a set period, so a **lower duty cycle** permits more power while on.

<Averaging />

<Key>Heating, not poisoning. Half the time on air, double the power allowed.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="evaluation" title="Over 1 mW average? Check the exemption, or evaluate" qs="G0A03 G0A06 G0A09 G0A12">

Rules apply to **all stations above 1 mW time-averaged**. If you **fail the exemption criteria**, evaluate per **OET Bulletin 65**. Any of these works: **calculation, computer modeling, or measurement** with calibrated equipment. To measure, use a **calibrated field strength meter with a calibrated antenna**.

<Evaluation />

<Key>Exempt, or evaluate by calculation, model or calibrated measurement.</Key>

</Concept>

<Concept id="exposure-zone" title="Over the limit? Keep people out of the beam" qs="G0A05 G0A08 G0A10 G0A11">

Do a **routine evaluation**, then **prevent access to any high-exposure area**. If a neighbor could be in a directional antenna's **main lobe**, make sure it **cannot point at them when they are present**. For an **indoor antenna**, make sure MPE limits are not exceeded in occupied areas.

<ExposureZone />

<Key>Evaluate, then control access. No filings or permission slips.</Key>

</Concept>
